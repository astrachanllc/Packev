Tilt Rips: 60 packs, no published odds

Tilt Rips has the largest pack catalogue of any platform we track, prices starting at ten cents, and the best cash-out terms we have verified. It publishes no pull rates for any of it.

Across roughly 60 packs priced from $0.10 to $3,000, Tilt Rips publishes no pull rates, no value tiers and no expected value. Its sell-back is the strongest we have verified — 90% of market value, withdrawable in dollars or crypto — but the platform sets that market value itself, does not name its data source, and states the price cannot be disputed.

The best cash route in the category

On every other platform we have examined, the headline buyback rate erodes somewhere: Fanatics deducts a 6% marketplace fee and pays in credit you cannot withdraw, Packz pays credit that can only buy more packs, Courtyard's advertised 90% has been independently measured nearer 85%. Tilt Rips pays 90% of market value into a wallet you can withdraw from in US dollars or several cryptocurrencies, with no fees or commissions beyond those shown in the app.

Taken at face value that is the strongest sell-back we have been able to verify. Fast Rips advertises 100%, but its own terms describe non-withdrawable credit, so we do not count it. Crown Rips claims 100% but is unverified and in closed beta; Arena Club's 90% to a Stripe-connected bank account is the closest comparison and is equally good. Tilt Rips also vaults cards free with no storage fees and no time limit, so holding a card costs nothing.

The catch is who decides what 90% is 90% of

Tilt Rips says the price is set by live market data, but it does not name the data source, and it states plainly that the sell-back price cannot be disputed. A rate is only as good as the valuation it is applied to, and here the party paying you also sets that valuation, from a source you cannot inspect, with no appeal.

Fanatics is the useful contrast: it pays a worse effective rate, 84.6% against 90%, but names Card Ladder as its valuation source, so the number can be checked against data the buyer can also reach. A verifiable 84.6% and an unverifiable 90% are not obviously ranked in the order the percentages suggest.

The odds gap

Pack pages show a list of top prizes with market values attached — on one $100 pack those run as high as $90,000, which is 900 times the pack price. A ceiling is the easiest thing in this business to advertise and the least useful thing to know. What it does not come with is any probability, so there is no way to turn it into an expected value.

The site also makes two claims that read as transparency but are not odds disclosure: that every pack contains real physical cards held with security partners, and that pricing comes from live market data. Both speak to inventory and valuation. Neither tells you your chance of pulling anything. We found no provably-fair mechanism, no third-party audit of pull rates, and no published odds of any kind.

With roughly 60 packs, Tilt Rips has more products with no disclosed odds than any other platform we track has products at all.

Frequently asked

Does Tilt Rips publish pack odds?

No. Across roughly 60 packs we found no pull rates, drop rates, probabilities, value tiers or expected value figures anywhere on the site. Pack pages show a list of top prizes with their market values, which tells you what the ceiling looks like but nothing about your chance of reaching it. Tilt Rips has the largest pack catalogue of any platform we track and the least odds disclosure.

Can you withdraw money from Tilt Rips?

Yes, and this is the platform's strongest feature. Selling a card back pays 90% of market value into a Tilt Rips wallet, and withdrawals are available in US dollars and several cryptocurrencies, with no fees or commissions beyond those shown in the app. That is a better cash route than Fanatics, which pays 84.6% effective in non-withdrawable credit, and Packz, which pays 90% in credit that can only buy more packs.

How does Tilt Rips decide what a card is worth?

The site says the price is set by live market data but does not name the data source, and it states that the sell-back price cannot be disputed. Those two facts together are the counterweight to the 90% headline: the party paying you sets the number it is paying 90% of, from a source it does not identify, with no appeal. Compare Fanatics, which names Card Ladder as its valuation source, so the figure can in principle be checked against data a buyer can also see.

What is the cheapest Tilt Rips pack?

A $0.10 Dime Pack, followed by a $1 Dollar Pack and a $2.50 Rookie Pack. Those are by a wide margin the cheapest entry points of any platform we track, where the next cheapest anywhere is Crown Rips at $1 and Packz at $5. The catalogue runs up to $3,000 Grandmaster packs.

How long does Tilt Rips shipping take?

Up to six weeks, per the site, with shipping fees shown before you confirm and international delivery available for additional fees. That is the slowest fulfilment we have recorded: Fanatics quotes 3 to 15 business days. Vault storage is free with no storage fees, so there is no pressure to ship, but closing an account requires shipping or selling all inventory first.
