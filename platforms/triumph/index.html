Rips by Triumph: the biggest platform here, and its terms are MintPull's

A million-plus installs, a “100% BUYBACK GUARANTEE” in the FAQ, and a §5.5 saying Triumph is under no obligation to buy anything back. Its clauses match MintPull's word for word, section number for section number.

  • No odds published
  • Pays cash, not credit
  • Marketing conflicts with terms
  • Short clock

The FAQ is headed “100% BUYBACK GUARANTEE.” Section 5.5 of the Terms of Use says Triumph “is under no obligation to” offer a Return and may offer or end Returns at any time. The FAQ separately reserves the right to change the guarantee on seven days' notice, by editing the FAQ.

Read from docs.triumpharcade.com, the Triumph help centre and the Google Play listing on 2026-10-22. No pack prices or odds are published anywhere public.

The largest platform on this site

Scale first, because it changes what the rest of this page means. The Google Play listing shows 1,000,000+ installs and about 64,500 reviews averaging 4.5. LETSGO, the next largest we have measured, shows 100,000+. Triumph is an order of magnitude bigger than anything else here, which means the terms below apply to more people than every other platform on this site combined.

It is also, in several respects, better documented than most. It names its entity, publishes a state-by-state restriction list, specifies its withdrawal rails and caps, and requires identity verification with a liveness test. Those are real disclosures and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

We have written this page before, about Fast Rips and LETSGO. The pattern is the same: a confident number in the marketing, a discretionary clause in the contract, and nothing reconciling them.

In fairness to Triumph, a discretionary clause is not evidence that the buyback fails in practice, and 64,500 reviews at 4.5 stars suggests it generally works. The point is narrower and still worth making: the word “guarantee” describes something the governing document declines to guarantee. If Triumph intends to honour it, writing it into section 5.5 would cost nothing.

What this does and does not mean

It does not establish that Triumph and MintPull are the same company, and we are not saying they are. Triumph TCG, LLC operates under Delaware law; MintPull is Arcane Answers Studio Limited in Hong Kong. A shared template can come from common counsel, a vendor's boilerplate, or one operator copying another's public terms.

It does mean something for readers. When you see “seven-day storage limit” and “proprietary FMV that may not match published market values” on a third platform, that is not independent convergence on fair terms — it is one document propagating. The clauses that favour the operator are the ones being copied, and they are spreading faster than the disclosure practices on SwishX or Courtyard.

Two places the documents contradict themselves

Separate from the guarantee, we found two internal inconsistencies while reading. Neither is severe. Both are the kind of thing that gets fixed once someone points at it.

Tax thresholdThe FAQ says withdrawals pause once you exceed $600 in a year until tax forms are completed. Section 6.3 of the terms says tax forms may be required for withdrawals over $2,000 in a calendar year
Shipping insuranceOne FAQ answer says Rips pays the insurance when FMV is over $1,000. Another says the buyer must purchase it. Insurance is optional below $1,000 and required at or above it
Withdrawal speed§6.3: “within two weeks of the request.” FAQ: instant withdrawals average about ten minutes

That last pairing is familiar — Packed has the same ten-minutes-versus-two-weeks split. The terms set an outer bound and the FAQ describes the norm, which is defensible, but a reader deciding whether to leave a balance on the platform is entitled to see both numbers in the same place.

What it does disclose well

  • State restrictions, specifically. Nevada is excluded outright, and Illinois, Utah and North Carolina have the 50/50 pack feature restricted rather than the whole platform. That is the most granular state disclosure we have found anywhere in this category, and most platforms publish nothing at all.
  • Withdrawal mechanics, with numbers. $6 minimum, up to $10,000 a day at Triumph's discretion, Venmo capped at $4,000 per rolling 24 hours, four payout rails named. Compare Fast Rips, which advertises cash and documents none of this.
  • A named entity and a real address. Triumph TCG, LLC in the terms; Triumph Labs, Inc. at a Claymont, Delaware address on Google Play. LETSGO names nothing on its own site at all.
  • Identity verification is described honestly — 18+, government ID, a liveness test, usually 24 hours, with manual review and possible notarised documents if it fails.

If you use Rips by Triumph

Mind the seven days. The window is longer than most of this category, but it ends in an automatic sale at a value locked when you pulled the card, not at today's price. Screenshot the FMV at the pull. Verify your identity before you want to ship or withdraw, not after. And treat promotional cards as entertainment only — they carry no buyback value and can be removed.

If this has stopped feeling like fun, free and confidential help is available: call or text 1-800-GAMBLER.

Frequently asked

Does Rips by Triumph really have a 100% buyback guarantee?

Its FAQ headlines one. Its terms do not contain one. The FAQ is headed “100% BUYBACK GUARANTEE” and promises buyback at the fair market value in effect at purchase for at least seven days. Section 5.5 of the Terms of Use says Triumph “is under no obligation to” offer a Return, that Returns are final, and that Triumph may offer or end them at any time. The FAQ also reserves the right to change the guarantee on seven days' notice. Those are not the same promise, and the terms are the document that governs.

Can you withdraw money from Rips by Triumph?

Yes, and the rails are specific: debit card, bank account, or PayPal and Venmo subject to availability. The minimum is $6. Triumph may limit you to “no more than $10,000 in withdrawals in any single day” at its discretion, and Venmo is capped at “no more than $4,000 per rolling twenty-four (24) hours.” Section 6.3 says withdrawals process “within two weeks of the request” while the FAQ says instant withdrawals average about ten minutes. An instant-withdrawal fee applies; the amount is not published.

How long can you keep a card on Rips by Triumph?

Seven days. Section 5.3 states, in capitals, “YOU MAY ONLY STORE A COLLECTIBLE FOR A MAXIMUM OF SEVEN DAYS.” After that it becomes a Return at Triumph's discretion and you are credited the FMV as of when you received it. Triumph gives at least 24 hours' notice by push notification and may extend the deadline. Promotional cards are different: they carry no buyback value at all and may simply be removed after seven days unless shipped.

Does Rips by Triumph publish pack odds?

No. Section 5.2.1 requires a pack to show its producer, series or type, cost and number of collectibles, and says other details such as the chance of a given value tier “may be” provided. No figures appear anywhere public. The same section warns, in capitals, that “THE DISTRIBUTION BETWEEN RANGES MAY NOT (AND IS LIKELY NOT TO BE) EQUAL.” With no prices and no rates published, no expected value can be calculated.

Where is Rips by Triumph not available?

Nevada. Section 2.3 defines Eligible Locations as “currently the United States except the State of Nevada,” and Triumph's own help article confirms it. Three further states — Illinois, Utah and North Carolina — have the 50/50 pack feature restricted rather than the platform as a whole. That is the most detailed state disclosure of any platform we track, and it is on our by-state page.

Who operates Rips by Triumph?

The Terms of Use name Triumph TCG, LLC, trading as “Rips by Triumph,” under Delaware law. The Google Play listing gives the developer as Triumph Labs, Inc., at “2093 Philadelphia Pike Ste 4204 Claymont, DE 19703-2424.” Disputes go to individual AAA arbitration, by default in San Francisco, with a $225 consumer filing fee, a class-action waiver and a 60-day informal resolution step first.
