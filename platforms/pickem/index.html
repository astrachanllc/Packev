Pickem Ripping Packs: three hours to decide

Pickem pays 90% of fair market value into a balance you can withdraw to a bank card. You get three hours from opening the pack to choose between shipping the card and selling it back, after which it is sold automatically.

  • Odds published
  • Cash-out verified
  • Short clock

Three hours, and the clock starts when you open the pack rather than when you buy it. If it runs out, Pickem sells the card for you at its own fair market value. That is eight times tighter than Packed's one-day limit and the shortest window of any platform we track.

Confirmed across two independent reviews, read 2026-10-22. We have not verified the window first-hand and reporting indicates it is shorter in some states.

Why three hours matters

On most of this category, deciding what to do with a pull is a decision. Here it is a deadline, and a short one. If you rip a pack in the evening and go to bed, the platform has made the choice for you by morning.

The detail that sharpens it is where the timer starts. It begins when the pack is opened, not when it is bought, so a pack bought and opened at once gives you three hours from that moment. Shipping also requires an address and, on most platforms in this category, identity verification — which is time spent inside the window rather than before it.

Note what the short-window platforms have in common: they are also the ones paying the highest buyback rates. Pickem at 90% withdrawable and Packed at 100% withdrawable are near the top of the category on rate, and at the very bottom on time. That is not a coincidence. A platform that will reliably buy the card back does not want to be holding inventory, and the clock is how it avoids that.

What Pickem pays, and how you get it

  • 90% of displayed fair market value on a sell-back, rising to 100% on your first pack purchase.
  • Proceeds land in a USD balance and can be withdrawn to a bank card, with a $6 minimum and a $2,500 cap per withdrawal.
  • Only sale proceeds are withdrawable. Deposits and promotional balances are not, which is the same structure Packed uses.

That places Pickem alongside Arena Club and Tilt Rips in the group whose buyback reaches real money, and above Packz and Fanatics, where it does not. The full comparison is here.

Odds are shown, but only once you are inside

Each pack reportedly lists the cards it can produce with a pull percentage against each, the same per-card format Arena Club uses. That is real disclosure and better than the several platforms here that publish nothing at all.

Two things limit it. Nothing appears on the public website, so the figures are invisible until you have downloaded the app and are standing at the checkout. And Pickem offers Normal, High and Max probability settings at the same pack price, which means every pack is three distributions rather than one. Crown Rips has the same feature, and the same gap: no published figures for any setting, so a buyer choosing Max over Normal cannot see what they are trading.

Pack prices run from $1 starter packs to around $250, with a Pokémon Pro Pack and a Football Pro Pack at $100 and a Multi-Sport Elite Pack at $250. Prices do not change when you change the probability setting.

A social casino with a card product attached

Pickem Ripping Packs is a feature inside Pickem, a social gaming platform that also runs slots, table games and arcade games. Crown Rips is the same shape: a rip product bolted onto a sweepstakes casino.

The exclusion list is the tell. Michigan, Minnesota, Montana, Nevada and Washington are reported as blocked, and those are the states most active against sweepstakes-model operators rather than against collectibles sellers. Whatever the packs are, the platform around them is being treated as a gaming product. Our page on the legal position covers why that distinction is unsettled and being tested.

If you buy, decide before you rip

On a three-hour clock, the time to decide what you would keep is before you open the pack, not after. Know in advance what a pull has to be worth for you to ship it, and remember that the alternative is not holding the card — it is Pickem selling it at a value Pickem sets.

Frequently asked

How long do you have to decide on a Pickem pull?

Three hours, and the timer starts when you open the pack rather than when you buy it. If it expires, Pickem sells the card automatically at its displayed fair market value. Reporting indicates the window is shorter still in some states. It is by a wide margin the tightest decision window of the eleven platforms we track: Packed gives one day, Crown Rips reportedly seven, and Courtyard, Arena Club, Fanatics and Tilt Rips vault cards indefinitely.

What is the Pickem buyback rate?

90% of the card's displayed fair market value, rising to 100% on your first pack purchase. Proceeds go to a USD balance which can be withdrawn to a bank card, with a $6 minimum and a $2,500 cap per withdrawal. Only money from selling cards is withdrawable, so deposits and any promotional balance are not.

Does Pickem publish pack odds?

Inside the app, yes. Each pack reportedly shows the cards it can produce with a pull percentage against each one, in the same per-card style Arena Club uses. Nothing appears on the public website, so a buyer cannot see a single figure before downloading. Pickem also offers Normal, High and Max probability settings at the same pack price, which means each pack is really three different probability distributions, and we have seen no published figures for any of them.

Which states can't use Pickem Ripping Packs?

Michigan, Minnesota, Montana, Nevada and Washington are reported as excluded. The minimum age is 18, or 19 in Nebraska.
