What the numbers mean

The average is fixed; the shape is what changes. Nearly every pack returns the same 95%, whether you spend $15 or $10,000. What differs between packs is how that value is distributed. The chance of pulling a card worth at least roughly the pack price runs from 12% to 30% depending on the pack, so two packs with identical expected value can feel completely different to open.

Spicy packs trade the floor for the ceiling. The 5 Spicy packs start their lowest tier at around half the pack price instead of about 70%, and push their top prize far higher in return. If you want the lottery shape, that is where it is; the average return is the same either way.

Selling back costs you more than the posted EV suggests. Courtyard advertises a 90% instant buyback. Applied to a 95% pack that is about 85.5% of your money returned if you sell everything straight back. Independent tracking of recorded Courtyard pulls has measured actual instant offers nearer 85% of card value, which would put the real cash return closer to 80%. The posted expected value is the value of the card, not the cash you can get for it.

What we have not verified

The expected values on this page are Courtyard's own, calculated from Courtyard's own card valuations. We have not yet checked a sample of those valuations against real sold prices. If the valuations run high, the true return is lower than the posted figure. That check is the next thing we are doing, and we will publish it either way.

Odds disclosure

Courtyard shows each pack's odds as dollar ranges with a percentage against each, plus a single expected value figure. Most packs' odds sum to between 99.6% and 100.5% rather than exactly 100%, which is consistent with rounding the small tiers for display rather than an error in the underlying numbers. One pack in our collection, the Sealed Pokémon Booster ($50), appears to be missing a tier between $100 and $200 and needs rechecking.

Frequently asked

What is the expected value of a Courtyard pack?

Courtyard publishes an expected value on every pack, and on 79 of its 92 packs that figure is exactly 95% of the pack price. The $2,500 and $5,000 packs post 96%, and the $1,500 U.S. Coins Pack posts the lowest figure at 93.3%. In plain terms, the average card in a Courtyard pack is worth about 95 cents for every dollar spent, using Courtyard's own valuations.

Does Courtyard pay out in cash?

Courtyard advertises an instant buyback at 90% of a card's value, and the platform settles in USDC, a dollar-pegged stablecoin. Applying that 90% rate to a 95% pack gives about 85.5% of the pack price back if you sell every card straight back. Independent tracking of recorded pulls has measured Courtyard's actual instant offers nearer 85% of value, which would put the real figure closer to 80%. We have not yet verified the withdrawal path ourselves.

Which Courtyard pack has the best odds?

Every Courtyard pack returns almost the same average, so the meaningful difference is shape rather than value. The chance of pulling a card worth at least roughly the pack price ranges from 12% to 30% across the 92 packs. The highest posted return is the Pokémon Legend (Spicy) at 96.0%, and the lowest is the $1500 U.S. Coins Pack at 93.3%.

What are Courtyard's Spicy packs?

Spicy packs lower the floor in exchange for a much larger ceiling. They post the same kind of expected value as the standard packs but start their bottom tier at around half the pack price, and their top prize reaches far higher. The Pokémon Legend (Spicy) tops out at $79,900 against $40,000 for the standard Legend pack. Same average, more variance.
