Rip Station built a page for expected values and left every field empty

An “EV & Odds” page listing ten packs, every figure reading “Data unavailable.” An 83% buyback, the lowest advertised anywhere. And a FAQ unusually honest about what none of it guarantees.

  • No odds published
  • Non-US operator
  • Short clock

Rip Station links an “EV & Odds” page from its own navigation. On 2026-10-22 it listed all ten packs, from $25 to $500, and every expected value on it read “Data unavailable — inventory not verified.” Separately, the buyback is 83% of estimated market value — the lowest advertised figure across the twenty-four platforms we track.

Read from ripstation.io and its FAQ on 2026-10-22. Inventory-driven figures can change; this describes what the page showed that day.

We want to be fair about what this is. Rip Station's odds are drawn from the cards actually loaded in each machine rather than from a fixed table, and they move as cards are pulled and the vault restocked. “Inventory not verified” is plausibly a transient state while stock is being reconciled, and the page may well be populated when you read it. We are reporting a day, not a permanent condition.

But the structural point survives that caveat. Courtyard, SwishX, Gotya and CatchBack all publish an expected value that is there when you look. A disclosure surface that is empty at the moment of purchase is not a disclosure, however good the intention behind building it.

That looks damning until you read the rest of this site. Fanatics advertises 90% and nets 84.6% after fees. Fast Rips advertises 100% and its terms describe non-withdrawable credit. MintPull advertises 100% and pays pack credit with a $250 weekly cap. Triumph headlines a 100% guarantee its §5.5 says it is under no obligation to honour.

A plainly stated 83% may be worth more than several of the 100%s above it. That is the argument of our buyback comparison, and Rip Station is the clearest illustration of it we have found: the lowest number in the category, attached to the least spin.

The candour runs through the FAQ

Platforms in this category tend to write FAQs that sell. Rip Station's reads like a risk disclosure, and we think that is to its credit even where the content is unwelcome:

On the buyback“Not a guaranteed refund.” “Buyback is not guaranteed liquidity.” May be modified or discontinued
On fairnessPublished odds are “not a cryptographic proof of fairness”
On insuranceVault insurance is subject to policy terms and is “not a promise of full recoverability”
On valueFrom a linked post: “Estimated card value is not guaranteed cash profit”

Declining to claim provable fairness is particularly notable for a platform with Telegram and crypto-wallet sign-in, where that claim is close to standard marketing.

The question the FAQ does not answer

Withdrawals appear once, in the shutdown answer

The FAQ says buyback proceeds land “in your wallet.” It then describes no withdrawal process, no minimum, no cap, no payout method and no timeframe anywhere. The only mention of withdrawing money in the entire document is in the answer about the service shutting down: “available wallet balances may be withdrawn during that window.”

That tells us withdrawal exists. It tells us nothing about how to do it while the service is running, which is the question that decides whether an 83% buyback is worth 83 cents or nothing. Until we can confirm it, the cell on our withdrawals page stays unconfirmed.

Panama, under Delaware

Rip Station is operated by Byzantine Port Technologies Inc., a Panama company, as a subsidiary of Byzantion Inc., a Delaware corporation. The footer carries the Byzantion name; the Panama entity appears in the FAQ.

That is the fourth non-US operating structure we have documented, after Hong Kong, Israel and England and Wales. As we have said on each of those pages, incorporating abroad is not wrongdoing. It is a material fact about who holds your balance and which courts would hear a dispute, and Rip Station discloses it in its FAQ rather than burying it — which is more than LETSGO does.

Why this page has no rating

The platform's own expected-value page was empty when we read it, and no pull rates were published on any pack we could reach, so three of our five inputs are unavailable. The buyback rate is clearly stated at 83%, but we cannot confirm the payout form or the withdrawal route, so we cannot say what the 83% is worth. Frequently asked

Does Rip Station publish expected values?

It publishes a page for them. Rip Station links an “EV & Odds” page from its navigation, and that page lists all ten packs. When we read it on 2026-10-22, every single expected value read “Data unavailable — inventory not verified.” Not one figure was populated. The page says odds are published “when available” on each pack page. We could not find a populated set.

What is the Rip Station buyback rate?

83% of a card's current estimated market value — not of the pack price — within seven days of each rip. That is the lowest advertised buyback of the twenty-four platforms we track; every other platform advertises 83% or more, and most say 90% or 100%. Rip Station is also unusually clear that it is not a promise: the FAQ calls it “not a guaranteed refund” and says “Buyback is not guaranteed liquidity,” and Rip Station may modify or discontinue it.

Can you withdraw money from Rip Station?

We cannot tell you. The FAQ says buyback proceeds land “in your wallet,” but it describes no withdrawal process, no minimum and no cap anywhere. The only place withdrawal appears at all is in the answer about the service shutting down, which says “available wallet balances may be withdrawn during that window.” A platform that documents withdrawal only in the context of its own closure has left the most important question about its product unanswered.

Who operates Rip Station?

Byzantine Port Technologies Inc., a Panama company, as a subsidiary of Byzantion Inc., a Delaware corporation. That makes it the fourth platform we track whose operating entity sits outside the United States, after MintPull in Hong Kong, LETSGO in Israel and Gotya under English law. Sign-in is by Google, email, Telegram or X, and you can connect a crypto wallet.

What does Rip Station shipping cost?

The carrier's rate plus a $5 handling fee per card. Optional insurance is 1% of declared value with a $1 minimum, capped at $5,000 per box. The FAQ says “most single-card orders in the US come to about $11 in total,” and international runs “$18 to $36 per box, plus handling,” with duties extra. That is the most detailed shipping disclosure of any platform we track — most publish nothing at all.

Is Rip Station provably fair?

It says it is not. The FAQ states plainly that published odds are “not a cryptographic proof of fairness.” Odds derive from the cards currently loaded in each machine rather than a fixed pull-rate table, and update as cards are pulled and the vault restocked. For a platform with crypto wallet sign-in, declining to claim provable fairness is a notable piece of restraint.
