Fast Rips: the homepage and the terms do not agree

Fast Rips advertises a 100% buyback paid out to your bank account, which would be the best terms of any platform we track. Its own Terms of Use say Platform Credit has no cash value outside the platform and is not withdrawable.

  • No odds published
  • Marketing conflicts with terms
  • Credit only

The homepage says “100% FMV Buyback” and “Withdraw proceeds to your bank account.” Section 6.3 of the Terms of Use says Platform Credit “has no cash value outside the Platform” and is not withdrawable, and the Platform Statement describes no buyback programme at all — only an optional return paid in credit, “not a cash refund unless we explicitly state otherwise.”

The fair reading

We are not alleging that Fast Rips will refuse to pay anyone, and there is a plausible innocent explanation. The return option described in the terms covers Mystery Box contents and pays credit; the cash-out advertised on the homepage may be a separate flow for what the Platform Statement calls “Eligible Funds,” which it says may be withdrawable subject to verification, limits, delays and AML and KYC checks. On that reading both statements can be true at once and the terms simply do not document the cash path.

But that reading has to be inferred, and a buyer should not have to infer it. As published, the homepage makes an unconditional promise — 100%, to your bank account — and the governing document says the credit it pays has no cash value outside the platform and that withdrawals are at the company's sole discretion. The terms state that they control in a conflict, which means the document that wins is the one that promises less.

What to confirm before you deposit

Ask Fast Rips directly, in writing, and keep the answer: is the 100% buyback paid in withdrawable funds or in Platform Credit, what fees are deducted, and what is the minimum and typical processing time for a withdrawal to a bank account? If the answer is credit, the advertised 100% is worth materially less than the 90% that Arena Club and Tilt Rips pay into withdrawable wallets.

We have contacted Fast Rips for clarification and will update this page with their response.

Everything else is undisclosed

Seven packs are listed — Fresh, Basic, Pro, Bronze, Silver, Gold and Diamond — and not one shows a price. Every price field on the site renders as a dash. There are no odds, no value tiers, no floor, no ceiling and no expected value for any of them.

That may simply be because the product lives in the iOS app rather than on the web; the Platform Statement says that where odds are shown, “the applicable Odds are those shown at the time of your transaction,” which implies they appear somewhere. But a prospective buyer researching the platform before downloading anything can learn neither what a pack costs nor what is in it.

The language rule

One clause is worth noting on its own. Section 3.4 prohibits users from describing the platform as a gambling product, or from using the words “bet,” “wager,” “jackpot,” “stake” or “payout” when promoting it, unless the company approves. The Terms also assert that buying a Mystery Box “is not a form of gambling, a wager on a sporting event or contest, a lottery.”

Every platform in this category makes some version of that argument, and as our page on the legal position sets out, the question is genuinely unsettled and being tested in court. What is unusual here is contractually restricting what customers may call it. A company's terms cannot determine how a regulator classifies its product, and a rule of that kind is aimed at affiliates and reviewers rather than at regulators.

Frequently asked

Does Fast Rips really buy cards back at 100%?

Its homepage advertises “100% FMV Buyback” and “Cash Out to Bank.” Its Terms of Use set no fixed buyback rate at all, and its Platform Statement says there is no buyback programme as such. What the terms describe is an optional return for certain Mystery Box contents, paid in Platform Credit rather than money, and only “where applicable.” We could not reconcile the two, and the terms themselves state that they control in the event of a conflict.

Can you withdraw money from Fast Rips?

The homepage says “Withdraw proceeds to your bank account.” Section 6.3 of the Terms of Use says Platform Credit “has no cash value outside the Platform” and is not withdrawable, and the Platform Statement calls it “Non-cash, not legal tender, and for Platform use only.” Withdrawals appear only as a discretionary possibility: “If and when the Company, in its sole discretion, offers the ability to withdraw certain balances.” It is possible that cashing out is a separate flow from the credit return and simply is not documented, but as published the two statements conflict.

Does Fast Rips publish pack odds?

Not currently. No odds, pull rates or prices appear on its website for any of its seven packs. The Terms say odds are disclosed “where required by law or where the Company, in its sole discretion, chooses to do so,” and that odds “are subject to change over time.” The Platform Statement says that where odds are shown, “the applicable Odds are those shown at the time of your transaction.” Odds may therefore appear inside the iOS app even though none are published on the web.

How does Fast Rips decide what a card is worth?

It decides itself. The Terms describe fair market value as “a proprietary, Platform-administered valuation used exclusively for internal Platform purposes,” which can change without notice and carries “no guarantee” that it reflects any resale price. Since the advertised buyback is a percentage of that figure, the company sets both the rate and the number the rate is applied to.
