LastPack shows you almost everything — on a contract that contradicts its own help centre

Sell-back rate, min and max value, a posted EV and a full rarity table on every pack, plus a per-pull fairness check. Then §4.5 says your sell-back is pack credit while the FAQ says it is cash.

  • Odds published
  • Expected value published
  • Pays cash, not credit
  • Short clock

The FAQ: sell-back is “100% of fair market value, credited to your Lastpack balance,” cashable through Coinflow or USDC on Solana. §4.5 of the Terms: the credit is “in the form of additional pack purchase credits.” §5.3 then makes sale proceeds cashable after all. Separately, §4.3 caps storage at five days, after which the card is auto-sold at the FMV fixed when you bought it.

Collected from the live lastpack.com pack builder, its help centre and its Terms of Service (updated 16 October 2026) on 2026-10-22.

The pack page is the best in the category

Before you spend anything, a LastPack pack page shows the price, the sell-back rate, the minimum and maximum value in the pool, a GROSS EV percentage, every rarity band with its value range and probability, the top grails, and a live feed of what other people just pulled. A volatility selector lets you choose Steady, Balanced or Wild.

Two things follow. First, 95% looks like a design target rather than a per-pack measurement — the same figure on two different collections at two different volatility settings. That is the same architecture as SwishX, which says its packs are built to a 100% value target, and Gotya, which says expected value equals price. LastPack just sets the dial five points lower, and a pack built to 95% is an ordinary, honest piece of retail design.

Second, the posted figure is below what its own bands imply. At midpoints the bands work out to 136%; valuing the top band at its floor still gives 109%. That is the opposite direction from CatchBack, whose posted EV sat above its bands.

Why our number is the unreliable one here

The Grail band is the problem, as it has been on every platform publishing ranges. On the Pokémon Noobie pack it runs $8 to $500 — 62 times wide — and fires on 0.1% of pulls. Taking its midpoint assumes the average grail is worth $254, which is almost certainly wrong.

LastPack's own terms warn about exactly this, in capitals: “THE DISTRIBUTION BETWEEN RANGES MAY NOT (AND IS LIKELY NOT TO BE) EQUAL.” We think the posted 95% is likelier to be right than our midpoint estimate. We publish both because the gap is the honest description of what can be known from the outside.

The clause that contradicts the help centre

LastPack's sell-back is 100% of fair market value, displayed on the pack page next to the odds. That is the headline rate in this category, and the FAQ pairs it with a real cash-out: Coinflow for fiat, USDC on Solana through the website, two-factor required, with a cap of “no more than $10,000 in Eligible Sale Proceeds during any single period of seven (7) consecutive days” (§5.3a).

Then §4.5:

“Upon selling back to lastpack your lastpack Account will be credited the FMV of the collectible such credit to be in the form of additional pack purchase credits.”

Pack purchase credit is not money, and §5.2 is clear that “Purchase Funds are non-transferable and may not be cashed out, withdrawn, or redeemed for cash.” If §4.5 governs, the 100% never becomes cash. If §5.3 governs — it defines “Eligible Sale Proceeds” as amounts owed after LastPack buys an item from you, and makes those cashable — it does.

We think §5.3 and the FAQ describe the live product and §4.5 is stale drafting. There is a specific reason to think so, and it is the most interesting thing on this page.

Where that clause came from

We have now read this template three times. LastPack's §4.3, §4.4 and §4.5 are the clauses we published from Rips by Triumph and MintPull — the same sentences, shifted up one section number.

MintPull pays pack credit and the clause describes it accurately. LastPack appears to have built a genuinely better product — real cash-out, a 100% rate, provable fairness — on top of a document that still says pack credit, and added §5.3 to describe what it actually does without deleting the clause it contradicts.

That is the practical cost of the shared template we first wrote about on the Triumph page. It is not only that operator-favourable clauses spread. It is that a platform doing the right thing inherits a contract that says otherwise, and the buyer cannot tell which document governs.

Five days, at the price from five days ago

§4.3, in capitals

“YOU MAY ONLY STORE A COLLECTIBLE FOR A MAXIMUM OF FIVE (5) DAYS FROM THE DATE OF PURCHASE. THIS IS A STRICT DEADLINE — PLAN ACCORDINGLY.”

After five days the card is sold back automatically and you are credited “the FMV as determined at the time of your purchase.” Not today's value — the value fixed when you bought. On a card that has appreciated in those five days, the difference is theirs. LastPack may text you first and may extend the deadline at its discretion.

Five days is tighter than Triumph's and MintPull's seven, and the shortest storage cap we have found on this template. It is still far more generous than Pickem's three hours.

What it does well

  • Provable fairness you can actually check. An envelope fingerprint and pull number shown before you open, a receipt showing the ones used, and a verify button that recomputes the result step by step. Rip Station explicitly disclaims this; LastPack describes a working commit-reveal scheme in plain English.
  • It says packs lose money. §1: “Items may be worth less than the Pack price. Do not treat Packs as an investment.” The FAQ repeats it. That is Holofoil-level candour.
  • It disowns its own animations. §4.9 says the spinning wheel and reveal effects are “entertainment only” and do not affect the outcome, odds, FMV or buyback. No other platform we track bothers to say this, and it is the single most common misconception about these products.
  • If an item is missing, you get FMV plus 10% (§4.7). A platform that over-compensates for its own inventory errors is doing something unusual.
  • A responsible purchasing policy and self-exclusion, referenced in the terms and the FAQ. Only MintPull otherwise has one.

And what we would push on

  • Fix §4.5. One sentence currently tells buyers their 100% sell-back is pack credit. If that is not true, deleting it costs nothing and removes the single biggest ambiguity in the product.
  • No combined shipments. §4.6: “each individual collectible must be shipped separately.” On $1 packs, shipping a handful of cards will cost multiples of what they are worth. Poke-Collect ships an entire vault for $5 flat.
  • Title does not pass until shipment (§4.2). Compare Rare Candy and Rip Station, where you hold title to the card in the vault. Here, until it ships, you hold a claim rather than a card.
  • The valuation is still theirs. §4.4: FMV comes from a “proprietary system” and “may not match published market values.” Every number on this page rests on it.

Why this page has no rating

LastPack discloses enough to rate on odds, and would score near the top. We are not scoring it because the single most important term — whether a 100% sell-back becomes money or pack credit — is stated two different ways in the platform's own documents. That is not a detail we can average out. And as everywhere else on this site, nobody has yet tested whether these fair market values match real sold prices.

We would publish a correction the day §4.5 is updated. If you have cashed out from LastPack, or have a screenshot of a completed Coinflow payout, that settles it.

Frequently asked

Does LastPack pay cash for a sell-back?

Its FAQ and its terms disagree, and this is the thing to resolve before depositing. The FAQ says “current user-initiated sellback is 100% of fair market value, credited to your Lastpack balance,” and describes real cash-outs through Coinflow or USDC on Solana. But §4.5 of the Terms says that on selling back, your account is credited “in the form of additional pack purchase credits.” Pack credit is not money. §5.3 then contradicts §4.5 by making “Eligible Sale Proceeds” cashable. Two of the three say cash; one says credit.

How long can you keep a card on LastPack?

Five days. §4.3 states, in capitals, “YOU MAY ONLY STORE A COLLECTIBLE FOR A MAXIMUM OF FIVE (5) DAYS FROM THE DATE OF PURCHASE. THIS IS A STRICT DEADLINE — PLAN ACCORDINGLY.” After that it is automatically sold back and your account credited the FMV as determined at the time of your purchase — not today's value. LastPack may text you and may extend the deadline at its discretion.

What does LastPack show before you buy?

More than most platforms show anywhere. Every pack page carries the sell-back rate, the minimum and maximum value in the pack, a “GROSS EV” percentage, a full rarity table with value ranges and percentages, the top grails, and a live feed of recent pulls. A volatility selector — Steady, Balanced or Wild — changes the distribution. On both packs we collected, the rarity percentages summed to exactly 100.0%.

Can you verify a LastPack pull was fair?

LastPack says yes, and describes a commit-reveal scheme in plain language: your fairness page shows an “envelope fingerprint” and your current pull number before you open, your receipt shows the ones actually used, and after opening you can tap verify to recompute the result step by step. If that works as described it is the most user-checkable fairness claim in this category, alongside Clutch Packs. We have not run a verification ourselves.

Who operates LastPack?

Lotus Digital Inc., a Delaware corporation, under California law. Disputes go to JAMS arbitration in San Jose with a class-action waiver, a jury waiver and a 30-day opt-out. You must be 18, hold a personal US telephone number and a government ID, and pass KYC including a live selfie before adding funds, purchasing, shipping or cashing out. One account per person.

What does LastPack cost to ship?

Per card, and §4.6 is explicit that “the Company does not offer combined inventory shipments, and each individual collectible must be shipped separately.” On low-value pulls that will often cost more than the card. Insurance may be required above $100 and is offered without markup. Shipments go from California, with tracked international services available and duties payable by the recipient.
