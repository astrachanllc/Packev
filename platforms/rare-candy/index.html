Rare Candy: the best odds disclosure we have found since Courtyard, paid in a currency worth nothing outside the app

Every Instapack publishes five value bands with percentages and a guaranteed 50% floor, on the open web. Then section 8.1 says the Candy it all pays out in has no cash value.

  • Odds published
  • Credit only
  • Keeps by default

We collected full published pull rates for all 8 Instapacks — the first new odds dataset we have been able to gather since Courtyard and Packz. Every pack guarantees a floor of exactly 50% of its price. And §8.1 of the terms says Candies “have no cash or monetary value and are not redeemable, refundable, or exchangeable for” dollars or any monetary payment.

Collected from packs.rarecandy.com and rarecandy.com/terms on 2026-10-22. Rates are Rare Candy's own; card values are Rare Candy's own.

What they publish, and why it is better than the category norm

Ten of the seventeen platforms we track publish no odds on the open web at all. Rare Candy publishes, on every pack page, with no account and no app: five named bands, a dollar range for each, a percentage for each, a guaranteed floor, and a list of top hits with named cards, grades and values.

One detail makes this unusually honest. The bands are stated as total pack value, not per card. A platform wanting to flatter itself would quote per-card ranges and let the reader assume they stack. Rare Candy quotes the whole pack, which is the number that actually decides whether you are ahead.

The midpoint column averages 123%, and that cannot be true. A platform returning more than the pack price on every pack, on average, loses money on every sale. So the midpoint assumption is wrong here, and it is worth understanding exactly why, because the same flaw affects every value-range disclosure in this category.

The problem is the top band

Four of the five bands on each pack are narrow and well behaved. The Legendary band is not.

A band that fires on 1.5% to 3.6% of packs carries about 24% of the entire midpoint expected value, and it spans a range eleven times wide on average. On the Boosted Pack the Legendary band runs from $88 to $2,251 — twenty-six times. Where cards actually sit inside that range decides everything, and it is the one thing not published.

Value the Legendary band at its floor and keep the midpoint everywhere else, and the average falls to 98% — a touch under break-even, which is what you would expect a commercial pack to be. That is our best estimate, and it is an estimate, not a measurement.

This is the midpoint problem, stated plainly

Every expected value on this site that comes from value ranges rather than exact odds — Packz included — rests on assuming cards sit in the middle of their band. Rare Candy's data shows how badly that assumption can fail when one band is wide and rare. MintPull's terms warn about exactly this, in capitals: “THE DISTRIBUTION BETWEEN RANGES MAY NOT (AND LIKELY IS NOT) BE EQUAL.”

The fix is not for us to guess better. It is for platforms to publish the distribution inside the band, or an expected value, as Courtyard does.

The guaranteed floor is real, and nobody else offers one

Every Instapack page carries a guarantee: exchange the whole pack and you receive a stated minimum in Candy. We checked all 8 packs and the figure is exactly 50% of the pack price every time, with 100 Candy equal to $1 throughout.

Deep Blue, $4.99Guaranteed 250 Candy = $2.50 = 50%
Iconic Legends, $24.99Guaranteed 1,250 Candy = $12.50 = 50%
Modern Collector Gallery, $249.99Guaranteed 12,500 Candy = $125 = 50%
Only Slabs, $499.99Guaranteed 25,000 Candy = $250 = 50%

No other platform we track publishes a floor at all. Crown Rips discloses a per-pack minimum pull value, which is the closest thing, and its floor is far lower in proportional terms. A guaranteed 50% means the worst case is knowable before you pay, which is genuinely more than the category offers.

It is a floor in Candy, though, which brings us to the part that matters most.

Candy is not money, and the terms say so

§8.1 of the Rare Candy terms

Candies “have no cash or monetary value and are not redeemable, refundable, or exchangeable for” US dollars, cryptocurrency, outside goods, or any monetary payment. Rare Candy will not be “required to redeem Candies for cash or any monetary equivalent.”

So the exchange, described in the FAQ as a trade-in “worth up to 90% of the card's market value,” pays in a currency that only buys more packs. This puts Rare Candy in the same group as Packz, Fanatics, Fast Rips and MintPull — five of seventeen platforms where buyback proceeds cannot become money.

Note the phrasing too: section 3 defines the Exchange Price as “calculated up to 90% of the Item's Fair Market Value.” That is the same open-ended wording we flagged on LETSGO, and it means 90% is a ceiling rather than a rate.

What you can take out is the card. Shipping runs $9.99 for 1–10 cards, $17.99 for 11–25 and $32.99 for 26–50, with most orders shipping within 10 business days. On a $4.99 pack, taking delivery costs twice the pack.

Three things it does better than almost anyone

  • The clock ends in your favour. You have 48 hours to exchange; do nothing and the card is kept in your collection. Only Poke-Collect also defaults to you keeping the card. Pickem, Packed and Chalkboard all sell it for you.
  • You keep legal title. Section 9.2 says title to the card remains with you, with Rare Candy holding it as bailee in its Dallas vault, and storage is currently free. No other platform we have read states this. A card you own outright is a different asset from a balance on a platform's books.
  • The terms admit their own limits. Section 6.2 calls Rare Candy's valuations “inherently subjective” and says they are not appraisals or guarantees. Section 4.2 declines to warrant that the published card list is accurate. We would rather a platform publish numbers and be candid about their status than publish nothing.

And one defect worth fixing

The arbitration clause in section 18.1 requires a user to send a Notice of Dispute before arbitrating. The address it must be sent to is an unfilled template placeholder: “[insert physical or electronic mailing address]”. A second bracketed placeholder appears in the same section.

That is almost certainly an oversight rather than anything deliberate, and the practical effect is small because a user can contact support. But it is the gate to the only dispute process the contract provides, and it currently points nowhere. It belongs on this page for the same reason the rest does: it is what the document actually says.

Why this page has no rating

For once, the missing piece is not disclosure. Rare Candy publishes enough to calculate with, and we have. What we cannot do is pin the expected value inside the 98%–123% range the bands allow, because the distribution inside the Legendary band is not published; and we have not tested whether its stated card values match real sold prices, which is the measurement that would make any of these figures mean something.

If you have opened Instapacks and can share what you pulled against what the pack cost, that is the data that would close the gap. Our methodology explains why we publish a range rather than a single number.

Frequently asked

Does Rare Candy publish pack odds?

Yes, and more completely than almost anyone in this category. Every Instapack page carries a “Pull Rates by Pack Value” table with five bands, a dollar range and a percentage for each, plus a named list of top hits with their values. It is on the open web, with no app and no account needed. All eight packs we collected have rates summing to exactly 100%. Only Courtyard, which posts an exact expected value, discloses more.

What is the Rare Candy guaranteed minimum?

Every pack guarantees a floor in Candy if you exchange the whole pack, and on all eight packs we collected it is exactly 50% of the pack price. The $4.99 Deep Blue pack guarantees 250 Candy, the $499.99 Only Slabs pack guarantees 25,000 Candy, and 100 Candy equals $1 throughout. A published, checkable floor is something no other platform we track offers, and it is the most useful single number on the page.

Can you withdraw money from Rare Candy?

No. The exchange pays in Candy, and section 8.1 of the terms says Candies “have no cash or monetary value and are not redeemable, refundable, or exchangeable for” US dollars, cryptocurrency, outside goods or any monetary payment. Section 8.1 also says Rare Candy will not be required to redeem Candies for cash. Candy buys more packs. The only way value leaves Rare Candy is as a physical card you pay to ship.

What is Rare Candy's expected value?

Somewhere near break-even, but the published bands cannot pin it down. Taking the midpoint of every band gives an average of 123% of the pack price across the eight packs, which cannot be right — an operator returning more than 100% on every pack would lose money on every sale. The reason is the Legendary band: it is 13 times wider than its own floor on average and carries about 24% of the midpoint expected value while firing on 1.5% to 3.6% of packs. Valuing Legendary at its floor instead gives 98%. The true figure is somewhere in that range and depends entirely on where cards actually sit inside that top band.

What happens if you do nothing after opening a Rare Candy pack?

You keep the card. The FAQ says you have 48 hours to exchange a card, and if you do not, it is automatically kept in your collection. That makes Rare Candy the second platform we track, after Poke-Collect, where inaction leaves you holding the card rather than a balance. Pickem, Packed and Chalkboard all sell the card for you when their clocks run out.

Who operates Rare Candy?

Rare Candy Auctions, Inc., a Delaware corporation, with the vault in Dallas, Texas. Texas law governs and disputes go to AAA consumer arbitration seated in Dallas County, after at least 60 days of informal negotiation, with a class-action waiver. Instapacks are US-only and exclude Washington state. Rare Candy also runs a card scanner app and a marketplace; Instapacks is one product line of several.
