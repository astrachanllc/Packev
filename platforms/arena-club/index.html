Arena Club pack odds and buyback terms

Arena Club sells graded slabs in nine price tiers from $25 to $5,000 across as many as nine categories. It publishes detailed per-card odds and no card values at all, which makes it the hardest of the three platforms to evaluate before you buy.

Arena Club's expected value cannot be calculated from its own disclosures. It lists a hit rate for every individual card in a pack but attaches no value to any of them, and publishes no expected value figure anywhere. Its buyback terms, by contrast, are the best of the three platforms we have measured.

Collected 2026-09-21 and re-collected 2026-10-21 from Arena Club's pack pages and help centre.

How the odds are structured

Every pack page carries an "Available Slabs" checklist that lists each card with its own hit rate, grouped into three tiers. We collected two complete checklists, and both use the same structure:

98% of pulls come from the Lineup tier. On the September checklists that means a pack's expected value is almost exactly the average value of its 50 Lineup cards; the Chase and Grail cards barely move it. Your combined chance at any Chase or Grail card is about 2%, roughly one pack in fifty, and a specific Grail card is about one in five thousand.

The marquee names are mostly in the Lineup. A basketball checklist reads like a hall of fame roster, with Jordan, LeBron and Curry cards throughout. But Lineup cards have to be sized to fit the pack price, so at $100 those are common base cards rather than the ones a collector pictures when they see the name. The checklist is accurate and still misleading if you only skim the names.

Buyback and Collect Safe

Arena Club's standard buyback is 90% of the revealed card's estimated value, credited to an in-house wallet that can be withdrawn to a bank account or debit card through a connected Stripe account. Of the three platforms here, it is the only buyback that reaches real money without further conditions.

At checkout each pack also offers Collect Safe, an opt-in add-on costing an extra 10% of the pack price. With it you are guaranteed an offer of at least 80% of the pack price, or 90% of the card's estimated value, whichever is greater. On the $25 Pokémon Bronze pack the toggle reads “Guarantee a $20 Arena Club Offer, for just $2.50 more,” which confirms the premium is 10% of the pack price and the floor is 80% of the pack price before the premium is added.

So the add-on is worth buying if a pack produces a card worth under about 89% of its price more often than one time in ten, which is the break-even point for a 10% premium. Given that this is a product where the average card is worth less than the pack, that condition is very likely met. But Arena Club publishes no card values, so neither we nor a buyer at the checkout toggle can calculate it properly.

Arena Club grades some of its own cards

The checklists mix third-party slabs from PSA, BGS and CGC with cards in Arena Club's own holders, labelled with an Arena Club grade and a BAC certificate number. In the October checklist we collected for the $25 Pokémon Bronze pack, 11 of the 24 Lineup cards were Arena Club-graded rather than third-party graded.

This compounds the valuation problem. A PSA 10 of a given card has a public sales history that anyone can look up, so a platform's stated value for it can be checked. An Arena Club 10 of the same card has no established secondary market, so there is no independent way to verify what the platform says it is worth, or what it will offer you for it at buyback. A platform grading the inventory it sells and then setting the buyback price against its own grade is a structure worth understanding before you spend.

The checklist changed completely in a month, and the odds no longer add up

We collected the $25 Pokémon Bronze checklist twice, on 21 September and 21 October. Not one card appeared in both. The Lineup had also shrunk from 50 cards to 24, and cards disappeared from the list while we were reading it.

Taken at face value the October figures do not work: 3 Grail cards at 0.01%, 8 Chase at 0.25% and 24 Lineup at 1.96% sum to 49.07%, not 100%. Either the per-card rates are not updated as inventory sells out, or part of the list was not displayed. We are rechecking before drawing a conclusion, because the difference matters: if displayed rates go stale as cards sell, the odds on the page are not the odds you are playing.

Frequently asked

What is the expected value of an Arena Club pack?

It cannot be calculated from Arena Club's own pages. Arena Club lists a hit rate for each individual card but attaches no value to any of them, and publishes no expected value figure anywhere on its pack pages or in its help centre. Of the three platforms we have measured, Arena Club is the hardest for a buyer to evaluate before spending.

How do Arena Club pack odds work?

Each pack page lists every card available in that pack with its own hit rate, grouped into three named tiers: Grail, Chase and Lineup. Grail cards are listed at 0.01% to 0.02% each, Chase cards at 0.25% each and Lineup cards at 1.96% each. In the two packs we collected in September there were 3 Grail, 8 Chase and 50 Lineup cards, meaning 98% of pulls came from the Lineup tier. Because the rates are per card rather than per tier, you have to add up dozens of individual numbers to work out your real chance at anything.

Does Arena Club grade its own cards?

Some of them, yes. Alongside PSA, BGS and CGC slabs, Arena Club's checklists include cards in its own holders, labelled with an Arena Club grade and a BAC certificate number. In the October checklist we collected for the $25 Pokémon Bronze pack, 11 of the 24 Lineup cards were Arena Club-graded rather than third-party graded. That matters for valuation: a PSA 10 has a public sales history you can look up, while an Arena Club 10 of the same card has no established market, so there is no independent way to check what the platform says it is worth.

What is Arena Club's Collect Safe option?

Collect Safe is an optional add-on bought at checkout for an extra 10% of the pack price, which guarantees a buyback offer of at least 80% of the pack price. On the $25 Pokémon Bronze pack it is offered as “Guarantee a $20 Arena Club Offer, for just $2.50 more.” You pay $27.50 and cannot get back less than $20, so your worst case is losing $7.50, or 27.3% of what you spent. Without it the floor is only 90% of the revealed card's estimated value, so a low pull returns much less. The add-on pays for itself whenever the card is worth less than about $22.22, which is 89% of the pack price.

Can you withdraw money from Arena Club?

Yes. Accepted buyback offers are credited to an in-house Arena Club Wallet rather than refunded directly, and wallet funds can then be withdrawn to a bank account or debit card through a connected Stripe account. That makes it the only platform of the three we have measured whose buyback reaches real money without further conditions, as against Packz's credit-only buyback.
