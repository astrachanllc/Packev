Gotya says the pack is worth its price. We checked five packs, and it is.

Every odds table sums to exactly 100%, the midpoint expected value averages 100.5% of the pack price, and the 90% buyback reaches a real bank account. The free pack is the one that falls short of the claim.

  • Odds published
  • Expected value published
  • Pays cash, not credit
  • Non-US operator

Gotya's homepage says “each pack's expected value equals its price.” We took the published value bands from 5 packs and worked it out: the 4 paid packs average 102.4% of their price at midpoints. The free $10 pack averages 92.6%. The 90% standing buyback makes the round trip about 90%, which is the best we have measured anywhere.

The claim, tested

Platforms in this category make a lot of claims about value. This is the first one we have been able to test directly against the platform's own published odds on multiple packs, and it is the second platform — after SwishX — to design packs around a 100% value target rather than a markup.

The free pack is the exception

Gotya's free-pack page describes the offer as “the same $10 pack everyone pays for,” with the “same card pool, same published odds.” Its published odds are not the same shape as the paid packs.

At midpoints that is 92.6% of the nominal $10, against 102.4% on the paid packs. The bottom band starts at $3 where the Rookie Pack's starts at 73% of its price, and the top band is a tenth as likely.

We are not calling this deceptive. A free pack costing the platform real inventory is reasonably a thinner pack, and Gotya publishes the odds that show it — which is the opposite of hiding it. But “the same $10 pack everyone pays for” is a sentence the platform's own odds table does not support, and the fix is to delete the sentence rather than change the pack.

What to watch: the 180-day clause

Section 30: dormancy

After 180 consecutive days of inactivity, your account becomes dormant and Gotya may convert your Vault Assets to Wallet credit at “currently 90% of Fair Market Value at the moment of conversion.” Gotya must send at least two email notices, the first at least 14 days before conversion.

No other platform we track has a dormancy clause. Gotya otherwise has no storage deadline at all — its free-pack page says there is “no deadline” for keeping a card in the vault — so this is the only clock on the platform. Six months with two written warnings is far gentler than Pickem's three hours. It is still a mechanism that turns your card into credit while you are not looking, and the 90% is applied at conversion rather than at the pull, so a card that has appreciated is converted at the higher figure and one that has fallen at the lower.

And the parts that are weaker than the odds

  • English law, Wyoming shell. The operator is VBR GROUP LLP, registered in England and Wales at 32 Kinburn Street, London, alongside Gotya Collect LLC in Cheyenne, Wyoming. Section 36 puts the agreement under English law. That is the third non-US-governed platform we track. The Wyoming address in section 39 carries the postcode “8200,” which is not a complete US ZIP code.
  • No way to dispute a valuation. Sections 2 and 14 make FMV “a good-faith internal estimate” by Gotya's own methodology, and section 24 lists “Disagreement with FMV” as a reason a return will not be accepted. Every figure on this page rests on a number only Gotya sets.
  • The terms promise less than the footer. The site footer says “Odds published on every pack page.” Section 13 says Gotya “may display” probability brackets and expected value, and that odds may update, be rounded, be delayed or become unavailable. The practice we observed is better than the contract requires, which is the right way round — but the contract is what binds.
  • The odds move. Every pack page says the odds are “calculated in real-time from the live vault inventory.” Our figures describe 2026-10-22; they are not a commitment Gotya has made about tomorrow.

Why this page has no rating

Gotya publishes enough to calculate with, and the calculation supports its own headline claim — which is more than almost any platform here can say. What is still missing is the same thing missing everywhere: nobody has checked whether a platform's stated card values match real sold prices. On a platform whose entire proposition is that the pack contains its price in card value, that question decides everything, and until it is answered a score would imply a precision we do not have. Our methodology explains why.

If you have opened Gotya packs and can compare a pulled card's stated FMV against a comparable completed sale, that is the measurement we want.

Frequently asked

Does a Gotya pack really contain its price in card value?

On the paid packs we collected, yes, and slightly more. Gotya's homepage says “each pack's expected value equals its price.” We took the published value bands from five packs and worked out the midpoint expected value: the four paid packs average 102.4% of their price, and across all five it is 100.5%. The odds on every one sum to exactly 100.0%. That is a claim that survives checking, which is rare in this category.

Is the Gotya free pack worth the same as a paid one?

No, and it is the one place the claim does not hold. The free $10 pack's own published bands work out to 92.6% at midpoints — about 10% below the paid packs. Gotya describes it as “the same $10 pack everyone pays for” with the “same card pool, same published odds,” but the odds table on that pack is not the same shape as the paid ones. It is still a free pack and still worth claiming; it is just not the equal of the packs you buy.

Can you withdraw money from Gotya?

Yes. The standing buyback is 90% of fair market value, credited to your Wallet, which section 20 describes as an internal ledger credit. Section 21 allows withdrawal to a verified bank account held in your own legal name, and Gotya's free-pack page puts the cash-out floor at a $25 balance with payouts “within a few business days.” The terms themselves state no minimum or maximum figures.

What happens if you stop using Gotya?

Your cards are converted to credit. Section 30 says that after 180 consecutive days of inactivity an account becomes dormant, and Gotya may convert Vault Assets to Wallet credit at “currently 90% of Fair Market Value at the moment of conversion,” after at least two email notices with the first at least 14 days beforehand. No other platform we track has a dormancy clause. There is no ordinary storage deadline, so this is the one clock on the platform — and it is six months long, with notice.

Who operates Gotya?

VBR GROUP LLP, a limited liability partnership registered in England and Wales at 32 Kinburn Street, London SE16 6DW, together with Gotya Collect LLC in Cheyenne, Wyoming. Section 36 puts the agreement under the law of England and Wales, with the English courts having exclusive jurisdiction for business users. That makes Gotya the third non-US-governed platform we track, after MintPull in Hong Kong and LETSGO in Israel.

Can you dispute Gotya's card valuation?

Not through any published process. Sections 2 and 14 say Gotya determines fair market value as “a good-faith internal estimate” using its own methodology, and may disregard data it considers stale, manipulated or unrepresentative. Section 24 lists “Disagreement with FMV” among the reasons a return will not be accepted. The only route the terms describe is emailing Gotya before starting legal proceedings.
