Crown Rips: pack prices, floors and what is not disclosed

Crown Rips is the card ripping feature inside Crown Coins Casino, launched as a 500-person beta in July 2026. It is the only platform we track that sells rips inside a sweepstakes casino, and the only one with a $1 pack.

  • No odds published
  • Short clock

Crown Rips publishes a minimum and maximum pull value for every pack but no pull rates at all, so its expected value cannot be calculated. On the figures reported from the beta its disclosed floor is the lowest of any platform we track: most packs guarantee only 30% of the pack price, against 50% on Packz and roughly 70% on a standard Courtyard pack.

The floor is the story

A 30% floor is low for this category. It means a worst-case pull on a $100 Gold pack returns a card valued at $30. For comparison, the lowest tier on a standard Courtyard pack starts at about 70% of the price, and Packz guarantees 50% on every pack of $25 and up. Crown Rips is closer to Courtyard's high-variance Spicy packs, which start at around half the price, except that it applies at every price point rather than as an opt-in.

The cheapest packs are the worst, again. The $1 Starter pack guarantees only 10% of its price and the $5 Basic pack 20%. This is the same pattern we found on Packz, where the $5 and $10 packs have materially worse floors than the rest of the range. On both platforms the pack a newcomer is most likely to try first is the one with the least protection.

A low floor is not automatically a bad deal. Floor and average are different things, and a pack can combine a low floor with a reasonable expected value if the middle tiers are generous. That is exactly what the pull rates would tell you, and exactly what Crown Rips does not publish.

Volatility settings

Crown Rips reportedly offers three volatility settings on each pack, labelled something like Normal, High and Max, where a higher setting raises the chance of rare cards and also the chance of the lowest-value pulls. No platform we track offers anything comparable, and as a feature it is an honest acknowledgment that variance is a separate dial from value.

It also multiplies the disclosure problem. Ten packs with three settings each is thirty distinct probability distributions, none of which has published rates. A buyer choosing Max over Normal has no way to see what they are trading.

Cards in the vault may be sold automatically after seven days

Reporting from the beta says a card left in the vault is automatically sold back at market value after seven days. If that holds, it is the single most important term on the platform. Courtyard and Arena Club vault cards indefinitely, so keeping a card is a decision; here it would be a deadline, and a card you wanted to keep could be liquidated at a value the platform sets while you were not looking.

We have not confirmed this against Crown Rips' own terms. If you are in the beta, check it before buying a pack you intend to hold.

Why a sweepstakes casino is doing this

Crown Rips is the only platform we track that sits inside a sweepstakes casino rather than operating as a standalone retail business, and that is worth understanding rather than treating as a detail. Sweepstakes operators are built around a promotional-currency model with a no-purchase-necessary route, which is the structure they rely on to sit outside conventional gambling licensing.

Dropping a rip product into that framework is a plausible answer to the legal exposure described on our gambling page, where plaintiffs have argued that paying money for a random prize of uncertain value amounts to an unlicensed lottery. Whether it works is untested, and it may cut the other way: a product already operating under a gambling-adjacent licence may find it harder to argue it is simply retail.

Reporting conflicts on a basic point here. Some coverage describes packs being opened with Sweeps Cash, the promotional virtual currency used for the casino games, while beta coverage describes players loading real cash in dollars specifically for Crown Rips. That distinction decides whether a pack has a dollar price at all, and therefore whether an expected value can ever be stated in dollars. We are not guessing at it.

Frequently asked

What is Crown Rips?

Crown Rips is a digital card ripping feature inside Crown Coins Casino, a sweepstakes casino. It opened as a 500-person beta announced on 27 July 2026, with selected existing customers given early access. Users open mystery packs of sports and Pokémon cards and can keep a card in a vault, have the physical card shipped, or sell it back.

What is the expected value of a Crown Rips pack?

It cannot be calculated. Crown Rips publishes a minimum and maximum pull value for each pack but no pull rates or probabilities, so there is no way to work out an average. The platform's marketing promises “no dead pulls” but publishes no numbers behind that. Crown Rips is one of five platforms we track whose expected value a buyer cannot determine before spending.

What are the Crown Rips pack prices?

Reporting from the beta lists ten Pokémon packs: Starter at $1, Basic at $5, Signature at $10, Bronze at $25, Silver at $50, Gold at $100, Sapphire at $250, Amethyst at $500, Ultimate at $1,000 and Diamond at $2,500. The $1 Starter pack is the cheapest entry point of any platform we track, where the next cheapest is Packz at $5.

Does Crown Rips really buy cards back at 100%?

Crown Coins has said users can sell cards back for 100% of fair market value, which would be the best buyback in the category against the 90% offered by Courtyard, Packz and Arena Club. We have not verified it. Two things need checking first: what Crown Rips treats as fair market value, since the platform sets that figure itself, and what form the proceeds take, because the phrasing that proceeds can fund future pack purchases is the same language Packz uses for credit that cannot be withdrawn.

What happens to cards left in the Crown Rips vault?

Reporting from the beta says cards sit in the vault for seven days and are then automatically sold back at market value. If accurate, that is the most consequential term on the platform and the one least like its competitors: Courtyard and Arena Club vault cards indefinitely, so holding a card is a decision you make rather than a deadline you have to meet. Confirm it against the current terms before buying a pack you intend to hold.
