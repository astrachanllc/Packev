MintPull: a 100% guarantee you can withdraw at $250 a week

MintPull advertises a 100% fair market value guarantee. Its terms credit returns as pack purchase credit, cap withdrawals at $250 per seven days, and are governed by Hong Kong law.

  • No odds published
  • Credit only
  • Withdrawal cap
  • Non-US operator
  • Short clock

Section 5.5: “The only funds available for withdrawal are those you have received from sales of your Inventory,” and the Company “may cap eligible funds at $250 per seven days.” At that rate a $2,500 balance takes ten weeks to withdraw and a $10,000 balance takes about nine months. The same section credits returns as “additional pack purchase credits” rather than money.

Read from mintpull.net, its Terms of Service and its Responsible Purchasing Policy on 2026-10-22.

The withdrawal cap

Every other platform we track either states no withdrawal cap or sets one high enough not to matter. Pickem allows $2,500 per withdrawal. Arena Club, Tilt Rips, Packed and Fanatics publish no cap at all. MintPull's is $250 per seven days, which is the lowest by an order of magnitude and the term most likely to affect anyone who does well.

Note where this bites. It is irrelevant to someone who loses, and severe for someone who pulls well — which is the opposite of how a cap is usually framed. A single good pull on a mid-priced pack can take months to convert into money, and during that time the balance sits on a platform whose terms let it change fees “at any time in the Company's sole discretion.”

The self-exclusion problem

MintPull publishes a Responsible Purchasing Policy, which is more than most of this category does, and it offers temporary suspension or permanent account closure. Section 3.2 then says that on exclusion “any remaining funds in your account will be permanently forfeited unless withdrawn prior to the exclusion taking effect.”

Put that beside the $250 weekly cap. A user with $2,500 in their balance who decides to stop has to keep the account open for ten weeks to get their money out first. A user with $1,000 needs four weeks. The tool intended to help someone step away requires them to stay for a month or more, or forfeit the balance.

We do not think that is deliberate. It reads like two policies written separately by people who never put them side by side. But it is the practical effect, and it is the kind of thing a responsible-purchasing policy exists to prevent.

If you use MintPull, withdraw as you go

Do not let a balance accumulate. At $250 per seven days, money you leave on the platform is money you have committed to a months-long exit, and the self-exclusion clause forfeits anything still sitting there. Withdraw to the cap each week rather than waiting until you want to stop.

If this has stopped feeling like fun, free and confidential help is available: call or text 1-800-GAMBLER.

Who you are actually dealing with

The site lists three support addresses at three unrelated domains, which is unusual enough to be worth following. The terms explain it:

OperatorArcane Answers Studio Limited, trading as MintPull — Kwun Tong, Hong Kong (§17)
Payment agentsStarpnet Studio Inc (United States) and YISEN BREEZE LTD (United Kingdom)
Governing lawHong Kong (§14.1)
FundingOnly after the Company verifies you are in the United States (§6.2)

So the product is sold to US buyers, funded only from the US, administered through US and UK payment agents, and governed by the law of Hong Kong. Every other platform we track is a US company operating under US law. If a dispute arises over a valuation, a withdrawal or a forfeited balance, the contract points to a jurisdiction on the other side of the world.

That is not an accusation of bad faith — plenty of legitimate businesses are structured across borders. It is simply a material fact that nothing on the marketing site tells you, and it belongs in any assessment of counterparty risk on a platform that holds your money.

Odds, and an unusually frank sentence

No odds, prices or pull rates appear anywhere on the public site. Packs are described only as running from “Basic Packs to Epic Packs.” Section 5.2 says packs may display the producer, series, cost and item count, and that odds details such as value chance, rarity or grade may also be shown.

The same section then says, in capitals: “THE DISTRIBUTION BETWEEN RANGES MAY NOT (AND LIKELY IS NOT) BE EQUAL.”

That is a more candid statement than most of this category manages, and it is worth reading carefully. It warns that where a pack shows value ranges, you should not assume the outcomes within a range are evenly spread — which is exactly the assumption our own midpoint estimates make when a platform publishes ranges without rates. It is a fair warning, and it is also an argument for publishing the actual distribution instead.

Credit where it is due

Two things here are better than the category norm. MintPull publishes a Responsible Purchasing Policy at all, naming the National Council on Problem Gambling and Gamblers Anonymous and offering self-exclusion — most platforms we have read offer nothing. And the all-caps warning about unequal distribution within value ranges is a more honest statement about pack mathematics than several better-funded competitors make.

Neither offsets the withdrawal cap. But a site that only reported the bad terms would be a worse guide than one that reports both.

No prices and no odds are published, so no expected value can be calculated. We have also not tested a withdrawal, which is the claim most worth verifying here. Frequently asked

Can you withdraw money from MintPull?

Only money that came from selling inventory, and only $250 per seven days. Section 5.5 of the terms states “The only funds available for withdrawal are those you have received from sales of your Inventory,” so deposits cannot be taken back out. The Company “may cap eligible funds at $250 per seven days.” The minimum withdrawal is $5, processing takes up to a week, and KYC verification is required. At that cap a $2,500 balance takes ten weeks to withdraw and a $10,000 balance takes about nine months.

Is the MintPull 100% FMV guarantee paid in cash?

Not according to the terms. The homepage advertises a “100% Fair Market Value (FMV) guarantee,” but section 5.5 says returns are credited as “additional pack purchase credits.” Pack credit is not money, and since deposits are separately non-withdrawable, the only balance that can ever leave the platform is proceeds from inventory sales — subject to the $250 weekly cap.

Who operates MintPull?

Arcane Answers Studio Limited, a Hong Kong company based in Kwun Tong, trading as MintPull. Two further entities act as payment administration agents: Starpnet Studio Inc in the United States and YISEN BREEZE LTD in the United Kingdom. Hong Kong law governs the agreement. That is why the website lists three separate support addresses at three unrelated domains.

How long can you keep a card on MintPull?

Seven days. Section 5.3 states, in capitals, “YOU MAY ONLY STORE A COLLECTIBLE FOR A MAXIMUM OF SEVEN DAYS.” After that it becomes a Return at the Company's discretion and you are credited its fair market value as of when you received it. The Company may give 24 hours' notice by push notification and may extend the deadline. That matches Crown Rips and is longer than Packed's one day or Pickem's three hours.

Does MintPull publish pack odds?

Not on its website. Section 5.2 says packs may show the producer, series, cost and item count, and that odds details such as value chance, rarity or grade may also be shown. The same section states, in capitals, “THE DISTRIBUTION BETWEEN RANGES MAY NOT (AND LIKELY IS NOT) BE EQUAL,” and that statistics change in real time with inventory. No figures appear anywhere publicly.
