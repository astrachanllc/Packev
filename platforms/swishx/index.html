SwishX: the pack is worth what you paid. The exit is what costs you.

SwishX posts an expected value on every pack, publishes the odds and target card values behind it, and says packs are “designed to a 100% value target.” We checked the arithmetic and it holds. Then the buyback takes 12 to 22% back.

  • Odds published
  • Expected value published
  • Pays cash, not credit
  • Short clock

Across the 5 packs we collected, the posted expected value averages 101.1% of the pack price — the only platform we track that does not price a margin into the pack. It takes the margin at the other end instead: the FAQ says buybacks “can vary between 85-90% depending on the pack purchased,” falling to “75-80%” on a re-quote after 24 hours.

Collected from swishx.co pack pages and FAQ on 2026-10-22. Expected values are SwishX's own, computed from SwishX's own card valuations.

What they publish

Most of this site is an exercise in working out what platforms will not tell you. This page is the opposite problem. SwishX publishes, on every pack page, with no account:

  • A posted expected value for the pack, in dollars.
  • Five tiers, each with a fair market value range and an explicit target FMV.
  • The odds for each tier.
  • The named card pool behind each tier — player, year, set and grade.

That last item is rare. Fanatics publishes a checklist with no rates; Arena Club publishes rates with no values. SwishX publishes both, attached to each other.

We checked it on every pack

It matches. Recomputing the posted expected value from SwishX's own target FMVs and odds lands within 0.9% of the posted figure on all 5 packs.

This is the first platform on this site whose posted expected value we have been able to verify from its own disclosure. Courtyard posts an expected value too, but does not publish the per-tier card values that would let you rebuild it. That difference matters: a number you can recompute is a commitment, and a number you cannot is a claim.

What this does and does not prove

It proves SwishX's posted EV is consistent with SwishX's published odds and SwishX's own card valuations. It does not prove those valuations match what the cards actually sell for, and nobody — us included — has tested that for any platform in this category. A perfectly consistent expected value built on optimistic FMVs would look exactly like this.

Where the money is made, in their own words

A platform returning 100% of the pack price in card value cannot make money on the pack. SwishX does not hide where it makes it instead. From the FAQ, under “How much of what I pay goes into the cards?”:

“Every pack on SwishX is designed to a 100% value target — built so the combined fair market value of the cards in its pool matches the price of the pack. The full value of your purchase lives in the cards you can pull, not in a markup on top of them.”

And under “How is the instant buyback amount determined?”:

“Buyback offers are set at a defined rate relative to the card's current fair market value — this can vary between 85-90% depending on the pack purchased. Note that after the initial 24-hour buyback period, you can request a new buyback offer, which will vary between 75-80%.”

So the model is explicit: no margin on the pack, a 10 to 15% margin on the way out if you sell within a day, and a 20 to 25% margin if you sell later. For a buyer who intends to sell everything back, the round trip averages 88% inside the window and 78% outside it.

The clock costs points, not the card

SwishX has a 24-hour window, which on most of this site is bad news. Here it works differently, and the difference is worth stating precisely.

What the deadline actually does.
Pickem3 hours, then your card is sold at Pickem's price
Packed1 day, then the card returns to Packed
Chalkboard Pulls24 hours, then an automatic buyback is processed
SwishX24 hours, then the offer drops from 85–90% to 75–80%. The card stays yours in the Vault and you can request a new offer whenever you like

That is a materially better design. Falling asleep on SwishX costs you about ten points on an optional transaction. Falling asleep on Pickem costs you the card. We have criticised short windows on five platform pages now; this is the version of the mechanic that does not punish inattention.

What we would still push on

  • The buyback is a band, not a rate. “85-90% depending on the pack purchased” means the rate is known to SwishX before you buy and not to you. Publishing it per pack, next to the expected value already there, would cost nothing and close the last real gap.
  • The odds sum to slightly over 100%. We measured 100.04% to 100.64% across the packs. This is almost certainly rounding — the posted expected values match our recomputation once the rounding is normalised — but a disclosure this good deserves rates that total exactly 100%.
  • The valuations are still SwishX's own. Every figure on this page, including the ones we verified, rests on SwishX's fair market values. That is the same unresolved question we have raised on all twenty-four platforms.
  • No operating company is named. The footer says “© 2026 SwishX” and the FAQ is “Powered by Swish Breaks.” We could not find a legal entity, a registered address or a governing-law clause. For a platform that discloses this much about its product, disclosing nothing about itself is a strange omission.

Why this page still has no rating

It is the best-disclosed platform we have found, and it would score well. But every score on this site is held back by one missing measurement: whether a platform's stated card values match real sold prices. SwishX's whole proposition is that the pack contains its price in card value, which makes that question more load-bearing here than anywhere else, not less. We will not publish a score until someone has tested it. Our methodology explains why.

Frequently asked

Does SwishX publish pack odds?

More completely than any platform we track. Every pack page carries each tier's name, a fair market value range, an explicit target FMV, and the odds percentage — plus a posted expected value for the pack and the named card pool behind each tier, down to the player, year and set. Only Clutch Packs, which publishes observed results rather than projections, makes a stronger claim.

Is the SwishX expected value accurate?

It checks out against their own numbers. We recomputed the posted expected value from the published target FMVs and odds on five packs and landed within 0.9% of the posted figure every time. That is the first time on this site we have been able to verify a platform's posted EV from its own disclosure. It is not the same as verifying it against real sold prices, which nobody has done for any platform here, including this one.

How much does SwishX pay to buy a card back?

The FAQ states it plainly: buyback offers “can vary between 85-90% depending on the pack purchased,” and after the initial 24-hour window a new offer “will vary between 75-80%.” Those are published bands rather than a fixed rate, so you cannot know your exact rate before buying, but a published band is more than any other platform here gives you.

What happens after 24 hours on SwishX?

Nothing is taken from you. Unlike Pickem, Packed and Chalkboard, SwishX does not sell your card when the clock runs out. The card stays in your Vault and you can request a fresh buyback offer at any time — it is just worth less, at 75-80% instead of 85-90%. The deadline costs you about ten points rather than the card.

Can you withdraw money from SwishX?

Yes. The FAQ says withdrawals are “processed to your linked payout method and verified through Stripe,” from the Wallet. That puts SwishX among the minority of platforms we track where buyback proceeds become money rather than credit.

Who operates SwishX, and who can use it?

The site names no legal entity — the footer reads “© 2026 SwishX” and the FAQ page is marked “Powered by Swish Breaks.” You must be 18 or older and may hold only one account. No state restrictions are published anywhere we could find.
