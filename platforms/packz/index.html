69.6% of pulls are worth less than you paid. That is the first two tiers combined. The top tier hits once in a thousand packs. Most of the expected value sits in the common tiers rather than the chase, which is actually a point in the template's favour: the return does not depend on a jackpot you will almost certainly never hit.

The cheap packs are the worst deal

The $5 Common Pack and $10 Uncommon Pack break the pattern. Their lowest tier starts at 30% and 40% of the pack price respectively, instead of the 50% floor used on every larger pack, which drags their return below the rest. The packs most likely to be bought by a beginner testing the platform are the packs that give back the least.

Buyback pays in credit, not cash

Packz advertises a 90% buyback, and a new user's first pack qualifies for 100% capped at $100. What the headline does not say is that sell-backs are paid as site credit that cannot be withdrawn. The credit can only buy more packs. Your only routes to actual money are shipping the card and selling it yourself, or keeping it.

That distinction matters most on cheap pulls. Shipping a $2 card home to resell it will usually cost more than the card is worth, so in practice a low pull on a small pack leaves you choosing between re-ripping and keeping something you did not want.

Why this estimate has a band around it

Packz publishes value ranges rather than exact card values, so an exact expected value cannot be calculated from its pages. The headline figures here assume cards sit at the middle of their range. Real card values tend to cluster toward the bottom of a range, because cheap cards are more numerous than expensive ones, so the true return is likely at or below our midpoint estimate. Frequently asked

What is the expected value of a Packz pack?

Packz does not publish an expected value, but it does publish dollar-range odds on every pack. Working those out at the middle of each range gives about 100% of the pack price on every pack of $25 and up. The two cheapest packs are worse: the $10 Pokémon Uncommon Pack returns about 98% and the $5 Pokémon Common Pack about 92%. Because Packz shows ranges rather than exact values, every figure is an estimate with a band around it rather than a precise number.

Can you cash out a Packz buyback?

No. Packz pays sell-backs as site credit that cannot be withdrawn, so the only way to take value out of the platform is to have the card shipped and sell it yourself. The standard rate is 90% of fair market value, and a new user's first pack qualifies for a 100% buyback capped at $100. Several ranking sites score Packz highly on the strength of that buyback without noting that it pays in credit rather than cash.

Do all Packz packs have the same odds?

Yes. All 14 packs we collected use the same seven probabilities, 44%, 25.6%, 19%, 9.5%, 1.5%, 0.3% and 0.1%, with the dollar ranges scaled to the pack price. For every pack of $25 and up the tiers are exactly 0.5 to 0.7, 0.7 to 1, 1 to 1.5, 1.5 to 2.5, 2.5 to 5, 5 to 10 and 10 to 20 times the price. A $25 Starter Pack and a $1,000 Champion Pack therefore have the same expected return.

What are the chances of beating the pack price on Packz?

30.4%, on every pack. That is the sum of the top five tiers, which all start at or above the pack price. Put the other way, 69.6% of pulls are worth less than you paid.
