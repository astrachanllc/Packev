Poke-Collect: the only platform whose clock ends with you keeping the card

Every other platform we track runs a timer that ends in a forced sale. Poke-Collect's vault runs for a year and then ships the card to you, free. It also has no instant buyback at all, which is the trade.

  • Odds published
  • Card ships by default
  • Physical storefront

“Any cards still in your vault after one year are shipped to you automatically for free.” That one sentence inverts the structure of this entire category. Pickem sells your card after three hours, Packed reclaims it after one day, Chalkboard buys it back automatically after 24 hours, MintPull returns it after seven days. Here, doing nothing ends with a card in the post.

Read from poke-collect.com and its digital mystery packs pages on 2026-10-22. Tier odds are published on the pool page but render in-browser; we have not collected them yet.

The clock, inverted

We have written about decision windows on nearly every platform page here, always as a risk. The window is short, the default is a forced sale at a price the platform sets, and the user who goes to bed loses the choice. Poke-Collect is the first exception we have found, and it is worth being precise about why it matters.

Read the last row against the first three. On Pickem, Packed and Chalkboard, the platform ends the year owning the card and you holding a balance. On Poke-Collect, you end the year holding the card. Those are opposite businesses wearing similar interfaces.

The reason is structural rather than generous. Poke-Collect is a card shop that sells digital packs. The others are platforms built around buying cards back, and a platform that reliably buys cards back cannot afford to hold inventory indefinitely — which is exactly the point we made on the Pickem and Chalkboard pages. The clock is how a buyback business manages its float. Poke-Collect has no buyback business to manage.

What you give up: there is no instant sell-back

This is the other half of the trade, and it is a real cost. On every other platform here, a card you do not want converts to a balance in one tap. On Poke-Collect there is no such button on the pack.

What exists instead is a conventional retail buylist — “Scan & Sell (Beta),” offering “Instant Offers,” paid in “Store Credit or PayPal,” with cards “Reviewed Within 24 Hours.” That is a human-reviewed purchase of a physical card, not an algorithmic instant offer against a vault position. No percentage of value is published anywhere.

Which of those is better depends entirely on what you are doing. If you are ripping to flip — buying packs intending to sell every card straight back — Poke-Collect is the worst platform here, because the thing you need does not exist as a product. If you are ripping to collect, it is the only one that does not quietly push you toward selling.

That distinction runs through this whole site. A 100% buyback is a feature for a flipper and an irrelevance for a collector, and the platforms with the most generous buybacks are also the ones with the least patience for anyone who wants to keep what they pulled.

Odds: published, but we have not collected them

The pack pages link to a pool page that lists the cards and, in the site's own words, shows “each tier's chance per pack” across four tiers — Poke, Great, Ultra and Master. The product pages describe these as “live odds.”

That is a real disclosure and it puts Poke-Collect ahead of Tilt Rips, Fast Rips, MintPull, Packed, Pickem and Chalkboard, none of which publish a number on the open web. We have not been able to read it: the pool loads in the browser rather than in the page source, so our collection tooling sees only “Loading the pool…”

That is our limitation, not their failure, and we say so rather than writing “no odds published.” If the pool also lists the individual cards, this becomes one of the few platforms where a genuine expected value can be calculated from public information — tier probabilities plus a card list plus market prices. Collecting it is on our list.

Where we would push back

Two things are missing that every platform in this category should publish, and the fact that Poke-Collect is a real shop does not excuse them.

  • No stated age restriction. Eleven of the twenty-four platforms we track require 18+ in their terms. A randomised paid pack sold to anyone of any age is the single most common criticism made of this whole category, and it is the easiest to fix.
  • No published buylist rate. “Instant Offers” with no percentage and a 24-hour review is less transparent than the 90% figures elsewhere, even though those figures turn out to mean six different things. A number you can check beats a number you cannot see.

Neither is a term that works against the buyer in the way a $250 weekly withdrawal cap does. They are gaps, and they are worth closing.

Why this page has no rating

The odds are published but we have not collected them, so no expected value can be calculated yet — and unlike most platforms here, that is a gap we can actually close. There is also no buyback rate to score, because there is no pack buyback. We will update this page once the pool data is collected.

If you have opened Poke-Collect packs and can screenshot the pool page with its tier percentages and card list, that is the single most useful thing anyone could send us right now.

Frequently asked

What happens to a Poke-Collect card if you do nothing?

You get it. The digital mystery packs page states that cards stay in your vault “up to one year” and that “any cards still in your vault after one year are shipped to you automatically for free.” That is the opposite of every other platform we track. Pickem sells the card after three hours, Packed reclaims it after one day, Chalkboard processes an automatic buyback after 24 hours, and MintPull returns it after seven days. On Poke-Collect, inaction ends with the card in your hands.

Does Poke-Collect buy cards back?

Not as part of the pack. There is no instant sell-back offer on the pack itself, which is the feature every other platform in this category is built around. Poke-Collect instead runs a separate retail buylist — “Scan & Sell (Beta)” with “Instant Offers” — paying in “Store Credit or PayPal,” with cards “Reviewed Within 24 Hours.” No percentage of value is published. So there is no one-tap liquidity, and no published rate to compare against the 90% and 100% offers elsewhere.

How much do Poke-Collect digital packs cost?

Four packs are listed. The Mystery Pack is $9.99 for one card from the Poke, Great, Ultra or Master tiers. The Japanese Edition is $9.99 for one card from the Japanese pool. The Booster Edition is $12.99 for five cards revealed one at a time with the best card last. The Gold Edition is $38.99 for one high-end Near Mint English or Japanese single. That is the lowest ceiling of any platform we track — most run to $2,500 or $5,000.

Does Poke-Collect publish pack odds?

Yes, on the web, which puts it ahead of six of the platforms we track. The pack pages link to a pool page that shows the card list and, in the site's own words, “each tier's chance per pack.” The catch for us is that the pool renders in the browser rather than in the page source, so we have not been able to collect the figures yet and cannot publish an expected value. That is a limitation on our side, not a disclosure failure on theirs.

Who operates Poke-Collect?

A Pokémon card shop with a physical storefront at 181 Hughes Road, Suite 5, Madison, Alabama, which also sells sealed product and singles online, runs live breaks on Whatnot, and operates a buylist. The digital packs are one line of an existing retail business rather than the whole company. It is the only operator we track with a street address you can walk into.

What does shipping cost on Poke-Collect?

$5 flat for your entire vault, free if you ship 25 or more cards at once, and free for local pickup at the Madison store. Shipping is US addresses only. Cards ship sleeved and top-loaded. Because shipping is charged per vault rather than per card, the cost of taking delivery falls the longer you accumulate — the reverse of platforms that ship one card at a time.
