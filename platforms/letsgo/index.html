LETSGO: the same buyback described two different ways

The website says 90% of fair market value. The App Store listing says “up to 90%.” The site names no operating company at all — and its own homepage says the platform may be illegal where you are.

  • No odds published
  • Cash-out verified
  • Marketing conflicts with terms
  • Non-US operator

letsgooo.com: “Sell at 90% of fair market value.” The App Store listing for the same app: LETSGO “buys back cards at up to 90% of fair market value.” A rate and a ceiling are not the same promise, and no floor is published anywhere. Separately, the homepage states: “The LETSGO platform may be illegal in the jurisdiction in which you are located.”

Read from letsgooo.com, its privacy policy, and the App Store and Google Play listings on 2026-10-22. We could not reach a terms of use document at any URL we tried.

“90%” against “up to 90%”

We have written this page before, about Fast Rips, where the homepage advertised cash and the terms described non-withdrawable credit. This is a smaller gap and the same species of problem: two official descriptions of one product that a buyer cannot reconcile.

LETSGO's buyback, as described in each place it is described, 2026-10-22.

This matters more than the five-point spreads we normally write about. Across the platforms we track, the difference between the best and worst advertised 90% works out to a few dollars per hundred of card value. The difference between “90%” and “up to 90%” is unbounded, because the second phrase sets no floor. A buyback that returns 90% on one card and 40% on the next is fully consistent with “up to 90%.”

We are not alleging that LETSGO pays less than 90%. Several app reviews describe buybacks working. We are saying that the company has published two different promises and a buyer has no way to know which is the real one — and that the one place this would normally be settled, a terms of use document, is the one place we could not find.

The operator is not named on its own site

This is the part we would want to know before depositing anything.

letsgooo.comNo company name, no registered address, no country. “© 2026 LETSGO” and info@letsgooo.com
Privacy policyNames “LETSGO and its affiliated companies” as the Company. No legal entity, no address, no country, no governing law. Header dated August 11, 2026; footer says September 2026
App StoreSeller: LETSGO INTERACTIVE LTD
Google PlayDeveloper: LETSGO INTERACTIVE LTD — “5 Hatidhar RAANANA, 4366507 Israel”

So the operator is identifiable, but only from Apple's and Google's records, which those companies require and LETSGO does not volunteer. That makes it the second non-US operator we have covered, after MintPull in Hong Kong — and unlike MintPull, which names its entity, its address and its governing law in its own terms, LETSGO discloses none of that itself.

Being incorporated outside the United States is not wrongdoing, and plenty of good products are built abroad. It is a material fact about who holds your money and which courts would hear a dispute, and a platform selling to US buyers should state it on its own website rather than leaving it to an app store form. We could not find a governing-law clause at all, which means we cannot tell you where a dispute would be heard.

The sentence on their own homepage

“The LETSGO platform may be illegal in the jurisdiction in which you are located.”

That is quoted from letsgooo.com, not from a critic. No other platform we track says this about itself. Pickem publishes a list of five states it excludes; MintPull names Nevada; several others say nothing. LETSGO neither excludes states nor claims legality — it puts the question back on the buyer and moves on.

We read it as honest rather than sinister: the legal position of paid randomised packs is genuinely unsettled, which is the subject of our is card ripping gambling page. But a warning with no list attached gives a buyer nothing to act on.

What it does well

Two things here are at the better end of the category, and both are real.

  • The cash-out methods are specific. “Paid to PayPal, Venmo, debit card or bank transfer” is a concrete list, and only Packed matches it for breadth. Four of the seventeen platforms we track pay credit that cannot be withdrawn at all; this is not one of them.
  • Cards are real and the grading is named. The listings describe professionally graded slabs from PSA, BGS or CGC with the grade and certification number shown, and state that nothing is simulated. A certification number is checkable against the grader's own database, which is a verification route most platforms here do not offer.

Shipping is slow, and reviews say so

A developer reply on the App Store says shipping “can take up to 21 business days” — roughly a month. Version 1.1.4 added identity verification before cards ship, which adds time at the front. For comparison, Poke-Collect ships a whole vault for $5 flat and Arena Club and Fanatics vault indefinitely.

Recent reviews on both stores report orders stuck in preparation and at least one unfulfilled refund. Those are unverified claims from individual users and we treat them as claims. We mention them because the 21-business-day figure is the company's own, and the complaints are consistent with it rather than contradicting it.

One reviewer also states the app “forces you to auto-sell if you hold it in your collection past 3 days,” which would contradict the homepage's “free vault storage” with no stated limit. We could not confirm it either way, and we are not publishing it as a finding — it is on our open questions list.

If you use LETSGO

Screenshot the buyback offer before accepting it, and keep the certification number on any graded slab — it is checkable against PSA, BGS or CGC directly, which is the one claim here you can verify yourself without trusting the platform. Ask support in writing whether the buyback is 90% or “up to 90%,” and what the floor is. Verify your identity before you want anything shipped, and expect the better part of a month.

Why this page has no rating

No pack prices and no odds are published outside the app, so no expected value can be calculated. The buyback rate is stated two different ways and we cannot tell which governs. No terms of use document was reachable, so storage limits, withdrawal caps, valuation methodology and governing law are all unknown. Four of our five scoring inputs are unavailable. Our methodology explains why we leave a cell empty rather than guess.

We have written to LETSGO asking which buyback figure is correct and where the terms of use can be read, and will update this page with any response. If you have in-app screenshots of pack odds, prices, or the terms, we would like to see them.

Frequently asked

Does LETSGO buy cards back at 90%?

Its two public descriptions do not agree. The letsgooo.com homepage says “Sell at 90% of fair market value.” The App Store listing for the same product says LETSGO “buys back cards at up to 90% of fair market value, paid to PayPal, Venmo, debit card or bank transfer,” and repeats “up to 90%” a second time. Those are materially different promises: a flat 90% is a rate, and “up to 90%” is a ceiling with no floor. Nothing we could reach states which one governs, or what the floor is.

Who operates LETSGO?

The website does not say. There is no company name, no registered address and no country anywhere on letsgooo.com — only “© 2026 LETSGO” and an info@ email. The privacy policy names no legal entity either. The operator appears only in the app store records: the seller is LETSGO INTERACTIVE LTD, and the Google Play developer record gives the address as “5 Hatidhar RAANANA, 4366507 Israel.” Every other platform we track names its operating company somewhere on its own site.

Is LETSGO legal where I live?

LETSGO does not say it is. Its own homepage carries the sentence “The LETSGO platform may be illegal in the jurisdiction in which you are located.” No platform we track makes a blunter statement about itself, and none publishes a list of states where it does or does not operate. We have no state-by-state restrictions to report for LETSGO because none are published. Our by-state page covers what the other platforms disclose.

Does LETSGO publish pack odds?

In the app only. The homepage says “Grails and odds shown before you rip,” the Google Play listing claims “transparent odds and real pricing,” and a version 1.8.2 release note mentions a “clearer odds display.” No figures appear on any public page, and no pack prices do either, so no expected value can be calculated from outside the app. One App Store reviewer reports opening 100+ packs with pull rates “in line with the stated odds,” while also saying they could not find an expected value per pack.

How long does LETSGO take to ship a card?

A developer reply on the App Store listing says shipping “can take up to 21 business days,” which is about a month of calendar time and the longest figure we have seen in this category. Version 1.1.4 added an identity verification step before cards ship. Several recent user reviews on both stores report orders stuck in preparation or unfulfilled; those are unverified user claims and we repeat them as claims, not findings.

How big is LETSGO?

Larger than most of the platforms on this site. The Google Play listing shows 100,000+ installs and about 1,050 reviews averaging 4.4; the App Store listing shows 4.0 from 8 ratings. Scale is not an endorsement — it means more people are exposed to the disclosure gaps below, not fewer.
