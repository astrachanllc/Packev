Pack ripping bonuses, ranked by what they are actually worth

Every sign-up offer we could verify across the platforms we track, with the arithmetic. One of them is plausibly worth more than it costs. Most are worth less than they look, and one platform that everyone lists as having an offer does not have one.

Clutch Packs' 20% first-pack discount is the only offer in the category that produces a calculable positive expected return: you pay 80% of face value for a pack whose observed average across 158,556 recorded pulls is 1.01 times face, which is about +26% on what you spend. It is also the only platform publishing observed results, which is the only reason the sum can be done at all.

All figures below come from published promotional terms and secondary reporting, read 2026-10-21. We have not verified any of them directly with the platforms. Bonus terms change without notice; check before you buy.

Correction

An earlier version of our research described a Courtyard referral offer guaranteeing a $25 buyback on a $25 starter pack for both parties. We could not substantiate that. Current reporting says Courtyard has no welcome offer at all, and its referral pays the referrer in points of undisclosed value. We have corrected it here rather than leave it standing.

Clutch Packs: the one with real maths behind it

A discount is worth more than a credit bonus, because it reduces what you pay rather than adding something you can only spend inside the platform. And because Clutch Packs publishes observed pull results, this is the only offer in the category where a discount can be set against a known return.

The pull-weighted average across all 25 packs is 1.010 times face value. Paying 80% of face for that gives an expected 1.26 times on outlay. Stripping out the jackpot inflation we identified on two of its packs and using a flat 1.00 times instead still leaves about +25%.

Three things that cut against it

The return is in card value, not money. This is the big one. Clutch Packs discloses no buyback or sell-back terms anywhere on its pack pages or its transparency page. A 1.01 times average in card value is only a 1.01 times average in cash if you can convert at par, and nothing published says you can. On every other platform this is exactly where the headline erodes: Fanatics pays 84.6% effective, Packz pays credit you cannot withdraw. Until Clutch answers this, treat the +26% as the ceiling rather than the expectation.

Roughly 63% of single pulls still lose money. The discount moves your break-even from a card worth 1.0 times face to one worth 0.8 times. On the Multisport Ascent pack, about 26% of recorded pulls cleared 1.0 times and perhaps another 11% of the 0.5-to-1 band clears 0.8. So about 37% of pulls beat the discounted price. A positive average and a likely individual loss coexist comfortably here.

The edge exists once. New customers only, one use per account, and it cannot be combined with other Clutch promotions. After that pack you are buying a break-even product with a 74% chance per pull of getting back less than you paid.

Conditions worth knowing

  • Card or debit only. Crypto, Zelle, transferred balances and promotional balances do not qualify.
  • No stated cap on the discount, so it scales with pack price — and so does variance. On the $2,500 Moonshot pack, 53.4% of recorded pulls came back worth under half the pack price.
  • New users only, 18+, eligible locations, one use per account.

The rest, briefly

Tilt Rips: a free $10 pack

Fund $10, buy a $10 pack, get a second $10 pack free, with phone verification required. Doubling your packs sounds like a 100% bonus, and in face-value terms it is. But Tilt Rips publishes no odds, no floors and no expected value for any of its roughly 60 packs, so the free pack's actual worth cannot be calculated. The efficient play is the minimum $10 qualifying purchase, since the free pack is a fixed $10 regardless of what you spend.

Packz: a better buyback on one pack

The first pack qualifies for a 100% buyback instead of the standard 90%, capped at $100. Since Packz's own published odds imply roughly 100% of face in card value on its $25-and-up packs, that extra 10 points is worth about $10 on a $100 first pack and about $2.50 on a $25 one.

The cap is the whole strategy here: a $25 first pack leaves three-quarters of the bonus on the table. One caveat we could not resolve — it is unclear whether the $100 cap applies to the pack price or to the payout, which changes the advice if you pull something large. And the payout is site credit that cannot be withdrawn, so the $10 can only buy more packs.

Courtyard: nothing

No welcome offer, no promo code needed. The referral pays the referrer up to 1,000 points after the referred user verifies identity and buys packs, capped at six referrals. Courtyard does not publish a dollar value for a point, and the new user appears to receive nothing. Several affiliate sites list Courtyard among their promo pages anyway, which tells you something about how those pages are assembled.

Arena Club: an add-on, not a bonus

Collect Safe costs 10% of the pack price and guarantees a buyback floor of 80% of that price. On the $25 Pokémon Bronze pack that is $2.50 for a $20 floor. It pays for itself whenever the revealed card is worth less than about 89% of the pack price — which, on a product where the average card is worth less than the pack, is often. We cannot put a number on it because Arena Club publishes no card values. Full analysis here.

Crown Rips and Fanatics

Crown Rips gives beta participants 5 Sweeps Cash to start, but the beta is invite-only and limited to 500 users. We could not verify any current Fanatics Instant Rips offer.

Read this before chasing any of it

Every offer here is new-customer-only and single-use. Whatever edge exists applies to one pack and then vanishes, after which you are buying a product whose average return is below 100% on every platform we have measured, and where roughly three-quarters of individual pulls come back worth less than the pack price.

A bonus that makes one pack positive does not make the next one positive, and "I'm up on the bonus" is the most common reason people keep buying. Decide your budget before you start.

Frequently asked

Which card ripping site has the best sign-up bonus?

Clutch Packs, on the arithmetic. A 20% discount on your first pack means you pay 80% of face value for a pack whose observed average return across 158,556 recorded pulls is 1.01 times face. That works out to about a 26% expected return on what you actually spend, and it is the only offer in the category we can calculate at all, because Clutch Packs is the only platform publishing observed results. The catch is that the return is in card value, and Clutch Packs discloses no buyback terms, so converting that card into money is an open question.

Does Courtyard have a welcome bonus?

No. Courtyard has no welcome offer for new users and no promo code is needed to sign up. Its referral programme pays the referrer up to 1,000 points once the referred user verifies identity and buys packs (sources differ on whether the threshold is $20 or $25), capped at six referrals. Courtyard does not publish what a point is worth in dollars, and the new user appears to get nothing, so there is no calculable value on either side.

What is the optimal first pack to buy with a bonus?

It depends on how the offer is capped. Clutch Packs' 20% discount has no stated cap, so the discount scales with the pack price — but so does the variance, and on its largest packs more than half of recorded pulls come back worth under half the pack price. Packz's 100% first-pack buyback is capped at $100, so a $25 first pack leaves three-quarters of the bonus unused while a $100 pack captures all of it. Tilt Rips' free pack is a fixed $10 regardless of what you buy, so the minimum $10 qualifying purchase extracts it most efficiently.

Are card ripping bonuses actually worth taking?

Each one is worth taking once, and none of them is a strategy. Every offer here is new-customer-only and single-use, so whatever edge exists applies to one pack and then disappears. After that you are buying the underlying product, where the average return is below 100% everywhere we have measured and roughly three-quarters of individual pulls come back worth less than the pack price.
