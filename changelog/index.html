What we have found, and when

A dated record of every platform we have collected, every figure we have published, and every change we have caught. Platforms revise their odds and terms without announcing it, so the date on a number matters as much as the number.

We date every dataset because this category changes underneath you. In one month, Arena Club's entire $25 Pokémon checklist rotated — not one card from September appeared in October, the common tier shrank from 50 cards to 24, and the displayed rates stopped adding up to 100%.

  1. 2026-10-23

    Correction: the “Cash-out verified” label overstated what we had done

    That chip appeared on eleven platform pages and said “verified.” We had read each platform’s terms and help pages describing a withdrawal path; we had not withdrawn money from any of them. The label now reads “Pays cash, not credit,” and its description says plainly that we have not tested it. No underlying figure changed.

  2. 2026-10-23

    SAJA added — the best headline terms, and the broadest forfeiture clause

    100% expected value and a 100% buyback, card values from third-party pricing services, ownership passing at the pull, a free vault with no deadline, and a cash refund when SAJA prices a card above market. The same terms let SAJA void every transaction and confiscate vaulted cards on suspicion, “without any obligation to investigate or to prove it.”

  3. 2026-10-23

    LastPack added — and the shared terms template turns up a third time

    LastPack posts a sell-back rate, min and max value, a GROSS EV and a full rarity table on every pack page, and lets you verify a pull afterwards. Its §4.3 and §4.4 are the same clauses we found in Triumph’s and MintPull’s terms. And §4.5 contradicts its own FAQ about whether sell-back proceeds are cash or pack credit.

  4. 2026-10-22

    Underdog Rips added, and Phygitals documented

    Underdog’s help centre gives a three-day window and no buyback rate. Phygitals, the third party that sources, vaults and ships the cards, documents 85% of FMV paid in USDC — and buyback windows of 30 minutes, 3 days or 7 days depending on card type. A 30-minute window would be the shortest on this site.

  5. 2026-10-22

    Rip Station added — an EV page with no EV on it

    Rip Station publishes a dedicated “EV & Odds” page. On the day we read it, all ten packs showed “Data unavailable — inventory not verified.” Its buyback is 83%, the lowest advertised figure we have found, and it is operated by a Panama company under a Delaware parent.

  6. 2026-10-22

    CatchBack added — the first platform selling side bets on the pack

    Publishes full band odds summing to exactly 100%, a posted expected value and the entire card pool with values. It also sells two optional wagers at $3.75 each on a $25 pack: a “Boost” that pays for itself only above $18.75 of card value, and “Insurance” capped at a $10.30 payout. Both expire in five minutes.

  7. 2026-10-22

    Holofoil added — the only platform with no cash exit at all

    §4: Holofoil Credit “cannot be withdrawn, cashed out, sold, or transferred,” and there are no cash payouts for sell-backs, vault conversions, promotions or returns. §10 converts vaulted cards to that same credit after 30 days. Shipping the physical card is the only way value leaves. Its §5 does contractually require pull rates on every product page.

  8. 2026-10-22

    Gotya added, and its central claim checks out

    Gotya says “each pack’s expected value equals its price.” We collected five packs: every odds table sums to exactly 100%, and the midpoint expected value averages 100.5% of price. The free $10 pack is the one exception, at 92.6%. Governed by the law of England and Wales, and §30 converts vaulted cards to credit after 180 days idle.

  9. 2026-10-22

    Rips by Triumph added — and its terms match MintPull's, clause for clause

    The largest platform we track, at 1M+ installs. Its FAQ headlines a “100% BUYBACK GUARANTEE”; §5.5 of its terms says Triumph “is under no obligation to” offer a Return. Its §5.2–5.5 share capitalised sentences and section numbering with MintPull's, verbatim.

  10. 2026-10-22

    SwishX added — the most complete disclosure we have found

    Posts an expected value on every pack, publishes per-tier odds with an explicit target FMV and the named card pool, states packs are “designed to a 100% value target,” and publishes its buyback band. We recomputed its posted EV from its own odds on five packs and matched it to within 0.9% every time.

  11. 2026-10-22

    Rare Candy Instapacks added, with full odds collected

    Publishes value-band pull rates for every pack on the open web, plus a guaranteed floor of 50% of the pack price. The first new odds dataset we have been able to collect since Courtyard and Packz. The catch: the exchange pays in Candy, which §8.1 says has no cash value.

  12. 2026-10-22

    LETSGO added

    The website says “Sell at 90% of fair market value”; the App Store listing says “up to 90%.” The site names no operating company — the Google Play record lists LETSGO INTERACTIVE LTD in Ra’anana, Israel. Its own homepage warns the platform “may be illegal in the jurisdiction in which you are located.”

  13. 2026-10-22

    Poke-Collect added

    The first platform we have found whose clock ends in your favour: cards sit in the vault up to a year and are then “shipped to you automatically for free.” Every other platform auto-sells. Also the only operator with a physical retail address. Tier odds are published but render in-browser; not yet collected.

  14. 2026-10-22

    Chalkboard Pulls added

    §7.1 pays 100% of FMV as withdrawable Buyback Credit — the best-documented cash terms we have read. §7.5 also makes Chalkboard the sole, “final and non-appealable” judge of that FMV, and the decision window is a single 24-hour period.

  15. 2026-10-22

    MintPull added

    Terms cap withdrawals at $250 per seven days and credit returns as pack purchase credit, against a “100% FMV guarantee” on the homepage. Operated from Hong Kong under Hong Kong law.

  16. 2026-10-22

    Pickem added

    Three-hour window from opening a pack before the card is sold automatically — the shortest in the category.

  17. 2026-10-22

    Packed added

    §6.3 limits storage to one day. Terms require Value Band odds to be displayed, which no other platform contractually commits to.

  18. 2026-10-22

    Fast Rips added

    Homepage advertises a 100% buyback paid to a bank account; §6.3 of its terms says Platform Credit has no cash value outside the platform.

  19. 2026-10-22

    Buyback comparison published

    Five platforms advertise 90%. One pays 84.6% after fees and three pay credit that cannot be withdrawn.

  20. 2026-10-21

    Arena Club checklist rotated entirely in one month

    Not one card from the September $25 Pokémon Bronze checklist appeared in the October one. The Lineup tier shrank from 50 cards to 24, and the displayed per-card rates then summed to 49.07% rather than 100%. Unresolved.

  21. 2026-10-21

    Clutch Packs observed results collected

    158,556 verified on-chain pulls across 25 packs, averaging 1.01× the pack price — the only observed return data in the category.

  22. 2026-09-21

    Courtyard and Packz odds collected

    All 92 Courtyard packs and all 14 Packz packs, collected first-hand and published as open data.

Open questions

Things we have published as unresolved rather than guessed at:

  • Arena Club's October odds summing to 49.07%. Either the displayed per-card rates go stale as inventory sells out, or part of the checklist did not load. We are rechecking.
  • Whether Clutch Packs has a buyback at all. No terms appear anywhere, which is the one gap in otherwise category-leading disclosure.
  • Whether Fast Rips' advertised cash-out exists. Its homepage promises a bank payout; its terms say platform credit is not withdrawable.
  • Whether any platform's valuations match real sold prices. Every expected value on this site still rests on the platform's own numbers.

Corrections

We publish these rather than quietly editing.

  • Courtyard referral offer. Earlier research described a Courtyard referral guaranteeing a $25 buyback on a $25 starter pack. We could not substantiate it. Courtyard has no welcome offer; its referral pays the referrer in points of undisclosed value. Corrected on the bonus offers page.
