What these columns actually mean

Four rows carry most of the decision, and they are not the ones the marketing emphasises.

  • Odds published. Four platforms publish something on the open web. The rest show odds only inside an app, or not at all. If you cannot see the odds before you pay, you cannot calculate anything.
  • Can the money leave. An advertised buyback rate tells you nothing until you know this. Four platforms pay credit that can only buy more packs, which is not money at any rate.
  • Who sets the card's value. On most platforms the company paying the buyback also decides what the card is worth. Fanatics is the only one that outsources the number to a third party. Chalkboard's terms call its own figure “final and non-appealable.”
  • How long you can hold a card. Windows run from three hours to indefinite, and on one platform the clock ends by shipping the card to you rather than selling it. The platforms paying the highest buyback rates have the shortest windows, which is not a coincidence.

Every platform in one line

  • Clutch Packs — The only platform publishing observed pull results rather than its own valuations.
  • LastPack — Shows the EV, the odds, the sell-back rate and a per-pull fairness check — on a terms template whose clauses contradict its own FAQ.
  • Courtyard — Publishes an exact expected value on every pack — the strongest disclosure we have found.
  • Packz — Full published odds on every pack, built from a single template — but the buyback is credit.
  • SAJA — 100% EV and a 100% buyback on third-party valuations — alongside terms that can void every transaction and forfeit your cards.
  • Holofoil — Contractually commits to publishing pull rates, then makes shipping the card the only way value ever leaves.
  • Arena Club — Every pull is a graded slab, with per-card odds but no card values attached.
  • Poke-Collect — A real card shop selling digital packs. The only vault that ships you the card instead of selling it.
  • Underdog Rips — A major fantasy operator entering rips on third-party rails that publish an EV, provable fairness and outsourced valuations.
  • Fanatics Instant Rips — The largest brand here, and the only one using third-party card valuations.
  • Crown Rips — A rip feature inside a sweepstakes casino, in closed beta. Lowest disclosed floor we have seen.
  • Tilt Rips — The largest catalogue and the least odds disclosure, with a good cash route attached.
  • Pickem — A rip product inside a social casino, with the shortest decision window in the category.
  • Packed — The only platform whose terms contractually oblige it to publish Value Band odds.
  • Chalkboard Pulls — The best-documented cash route here, on a valuation its terms call final and non-appealable.
  • Rip Station — Built an expected-value page and left it empty. The lowest advertised buyback in the category, stated plainly.
  • Fast Rips — Advertises a 100% cash buyback its own Terms of Use do not describe.
  • MintPull — A 100% FMV guarantee credited as pack credit, with withdrawals capped at $250 a week.
  • Rips by Triumph — The largest platform we track. Headlines a 100% buyback guarantee its own §5.5 says it is under no obligation to offer.
  • SwishX — Posts an expected value, the odds behind it and the card pool. The pack is worth what you paid; the exit costs 12–22%.
  • Gotya — Says each pack's expected value equals its price. We checked five packs and it does — then the clock is a 180-day dormancy clause.
  • CatchBack — Publishes the odds, the EV and the whole card pool — then sells you two side bets on the result.
  • Rare Candy — Publishes value-band odds and a guaranteed 50% floor on every pack — and pays in a currency with no cash value.
  • LETSGO — The buyback is described as 90% on the website and “up to 90%” on the App Store.

Frequently asked

How do I compare card ripping sites?

Compare four things, in this order. First, whether the platform publishes odds at all — only Clutch Packs, SwishX, Gotya, Courtyard, Packz, Poke-Collect and Rare Candy publish anything on the open web, and six publish nothing anywhere public. Second, whether the buyback money can leave the platform: eleven of the twenty-four pay into something withdrawable, six pay credit that cannot, and the rest do not say. Third, who sets the card's value, because a 100% buyback on a number the platform alone decides is worth less than 90% on a checkable one. Fourth, how long you can hold a card before the platform acts — windows run from three hours to indefinite.

Which card ripping site is best?

We do not publish a single answer, because the right platform depends on what you are doing. If you want to calculate before you buy, Courtyard posts an exact expected value on every pack. If you intend to sell every card straight back, Packed and Chalkboard Pulls pay 100% into withdrawable balances. If you intend to keep what you pull, Poke-Collect ships the card to you by default and Arena Club, Fanatics and Tilt Rips vault indefinitely. We leave platforms unrated where the data to rate them is not published.

Why are so many cells on this table blank or unknown?

Because the platforms do not publish the figures. Seven of the twenty-four publish no pack prices on the open web at all, and only nine publish odds there, so no expected value can be calculated for most of them. We leave those cells as “not calculable” rather than estimating. Our methodology explains why an empty cell is more useful than a guessed one.
